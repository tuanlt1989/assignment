# Nail technician theory bank — English master file.
# Original practice questions covering published nail-technology exam topics.

[n001] infection
Which federal agency registers the disinfectants used in nail salons?
- OSHA
* EPA
- FDA
- CDC
> The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) registers disinfectants. Use only products with an EPA registration number on the label, exactly as directed.

[n002] infection
Removing visible dirt and debris from a surface with soap or detergent and water is called:
* Cleaning
- Disinfecting
- Sterilizing
- Antisepsis
> Cleaning is the first level of decontamination. It removes visible debris and many germs, and it must be done before disinfecting.

[n003] infection
Which process destroys all microbial life, including bacterial spores?
- Cleaning
- Disinfecting
* Sterilizing
- Sanitizing
> Sterilization (for example in an autoclave) destroys all microorganisms including spores. Salons normally clean and then disinfect.

[n004] infection
A disinfectant used on salon implements should be labeled as:
- Fungicidal only
- Bactericidal only
- Sporicidal only
* Bactericidal, virucidal and fungicidal
> Salon disinfectants must kill bacteria, viruses and fungi. Look for these claims and the EPA registration number on the label.

[n005] infection
Multi-use implements such as metal pushers and nippers must be:
- Rinsed in water between clients
- Wiped with a dry towel
* Cleaned and then disinfected before every client
- Thrown away after each client
> Multi-use (reusable) items are made of hard, non-porous material. Clean them, then fully immerse them in disinfectant before each use.

[n006] infection
Porous single-use items such as orangewood sticks and most emery boards should be:
* Discarded after one use (or given to the client)
- Disinfected and reused
- Stored in a closed drawer for the next client
- Washed with soap and reused
> Porous items cannot be properly disinfected. Use them once, then discard them or give them to the client, as your state allows.

[n007] infection
Before placing implements into a disinfectant, you must first:
- Dry them with a towel
* Clean them to remove all visible debris
- Spray them with alcohol
- Sterilize them
> Debris shields germs from the disinfectant. Always clean first, then disinfect.

[n008] infection
How long should implements stay fully immersed in an EPA-registered disinfectant?
- 30 seconds
- 1 minute
* For the full contact time stated on the label
- Overnight, for every product
> The label states the required contact time (often around 10 minutes). Shorter times may not kill all the germs listed on the label.

[n009] infection

- Help digest food
- Live only in soil
> Pathogenic means disease-causing. Nonpathogenic bacteria are harmless or even helpful.

[n010] infection

> Staphylococci grow in clusters and cause pus-forming infections such as boils. Streptococci grow in chains.

[n011] infection
A localized infection is best described as:
- A fever throughout the body
* An infection confined to a small area, such as a boil with pus
- An infection that spreads through the blood
- A harmless skin discoloration
> A local infection stays in one area (redness, heat, swelling, pus). A systemic infection affects the whole body.

[n012] infection

- Cover the area with nail polish
* Not perform the service and suggest the client see a physician
- Apply extra disinfectant to the client's skin

[n013] infection
Standard Precautions require nail technicians to:
* Treat all blood and body fluids as potentially infectious
- Use gloves only when a client says they are sick
- Ignore very small cuts
- Clean only visibly dirty tools
> Many infected people show no symptoms, so every client is treated the same: gloves, proper cleaning and disinfection, and safe disposal.

[n015] infection

[n016] infection

[n017] infection

[n018] infection

- Once a month

[n019] infection

* Washing your hands thoroughly with soap and water
- Wearing perfume
- Keeping the windows closed

> Proper hand washing before and after every client removes most germs. Hand sanitizer does not replace washing when hands are visibly dirty.

[n020] infection
