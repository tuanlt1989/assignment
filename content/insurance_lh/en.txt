# Life & Health insurance producer exam bank (national/general content).
# Original practice questions. State-specific rules vary: always check your state's content outline.

[i001] basics
The basic purpose of insurance is to:
* Transfer the risk of financial loss from an individual to an insurer
- Eliminate all risk of financial loss for the insured, so that no losses ever occur
- Allow the insured to collect more than the actual loss so that the insured can profit
- Guarantee the policyowner a fixed rate of return on every premium dollar paid
> Insurance transfers risk. The insurer pools many similar risks so the losses of a few are paid by the premiums of many.

[i002] basics
Which type of risk is insurable?
- Speculative risk, where there is a chance of gain or loss
* Pure risk, where there is only a chance of loss or no loss
- Gambling risk
- Market investment risk
> Insurers cover pure risks (such as death or illness). Speculative risks, like gambling or investing, are not insurable.

[i003] basics
The law of large numbers states that:
- Larger policies always cost less per $1,000 of coverage, so insurers prefer to write them
* The larger the group of similar risks, the more accurately losses can be predicted
- Large insurers can never become insolvent, so their policyholders never need a guaranty fund
- All claims will be paid in full
> Predictable losses let insurers set adequate premiums. That is why they insure large pools of similar risks.

[i004] basics
For life insurance, insurable interest must exist:
- At the time of the claim only
* At the time of application
- Every year the policy is in force
- Only for business policies
> Insurable interest is required when the policy is applied for. It does not have to exist when the claim is paid.

[i005] basics
The tendency of people with higher risk to seek or keep insurance more than others is called:
- Moral hazard
* Adverse selection
- Morale hazard
- Indemnity
> Underwriting protects the insurer against adverse selection by classifying and pricing risks correctly.

[i006] basics
The four essential elements of a legal insurance contract are:
* Offer and acceptance, consideration, competent parties, and legal purpose
- A signed application, the first premium, a licensed agent, and a conditional receipt
- A policy form, an attached rider, a written endorsement, and a named beneficiary
- An insurer, an insured, a policyowner, and a beneficiary who has an insurable interest
> All four elements are required for the contract to be legally enforceable.

[i007] basics
In an insurance contract, the applicant's consideration is:
- The insurer's promise to pay the death benefit when a covered loss occurs
* The completed application and the payment of the initial premium
- The agent's commission
- The signed policy delivery receipt and the statement of good health at delivery
> Consideration is the value each party gives. The insurer's consideration is its promise to pay covered losses.

[i008] basics
Insurance contracts are called aleatory because:
- Only the insurer signs them, so only the insurer is legally bound by their terms
* The exchange of values is unequal and depends on an uncertain event
- They are drafted entirely by the insurer, so ambiguities are read in favor of the insured
- They can be canceled by either party at any time with written notice to the other
> A small premium may lead to a large payment, or no payment at all, depending on whether a loss occurs.

[i009] basics
Because the insurer writes the policy and the applicant can only accept or reject it, ambiguities are interpreted in favor of the insured. This makes the policy a contract of:
- Indemnity
- Utmost good faith
* Adhesion
- Conditional receipt
> In a contract of adhesion, the drafter (the insurer) bears the risk of unclear wording.

[i010] basics
An insurance policy is a unilateral contract, which means:
- Both parties make legally enforceable promises
* Only the insurer makes a legally enforceable promise
- The insured must pay premiums for life
- The agent can change the terms
> The insured is not legally required to keep paying premiums, but the insurer must pay covered claims while the policy is in force.

[i011] basics
Statements in an application that the applicant believes to be true to the best of their knowledge are:
- Warranties
* Representations
- Concealments
- Riders
> Representations need only be true to the best of the applicant's knowledge. Warranties are guaranteed to be true.

[i012] basics
Intentionally withholding a material fact that would affect the insurer's decision is called:
- Representation
- Waiver
* Concealment
- Estoppel
> Concealment of material facts can allow the insurer to void the contract, usually within the contestable period.

[i013] basics
The authority that the public reasonably believes an agent has, based on the insurer's actions, is:
- Express authority
- Implied authority
* Apparent authority
- Limited authority
> Express authority is written in the agent contract; implied authority is what is needed to carry it out; apparent authority comes from appearances.

[i014] basics
Premiums collected by an agent on behalf of an insurer must be handled in a fiduciary capacity. Mixing them with personal funds is called:
- Rebating
- Twisting
* Commingling
- Sliding
> Agents must keep premium funds separate and forward them promptly. Commingling is prohibited.

[i015] basics
The Medical Information Bureau (MIB) is used by insurers to:
- Pay medical claims directly to doctors and hospitals on the insurer's behalf
* Share underwriting information to help detect misrepresentation
- Approve doctors
- Sell health insurance policies directly to applicants who were declined elsewhere
> MIB information alone cannot be the sole reason to decline an applicant.

[i016] basics
Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, if an applicant is declined based on a consumer report, the insurer must:
* Notify the applicant and identify the reporting agency
- Keep the reason secret to protect the privacy of the consumer reporting agency
- Charge a higher premium without notice and keep the reason confidential
- Cancel all of the applicant's other policies with that insurer
> Consumers have the right to know when a consumer report is used and to dispute inaccurate information.

[i017] basics
A mutual insurance company is owned by its:
- Stockholders
* Policyholders
- Agents
- State government
> Mutual companies usually issue participating policies that may pay dividends to policyholders.

[i018] basics
An insurer incorporated in another U.S. state but doing business in your state is a:
- Domestic insurer
* Foreign insurer
- Alien insurer
- Fraternal insurer
> Domestic: formed in this state. Foreign: formed in another state. Alien: formed in another country.

[i019] basics
A conditional receipt given with the initial premium generally provides coverage:
- Only after the policy is delivered and the applicant signs a statement of continued good health
* From the application or medical exam date (whichever is later), if insurable as applied for
- Even if the applicant is uninsurable
- Only for accidental death
> If the applicant qualifies as applied for, coverage is effective as of the application or exam date.

[i020] basics
When a new life insurance policy will replace an existing one, the agent generally must:
- Hide the replacement from the existing insurer until the new policy's free look period ends
* Follow the state's replacement rules, including required notices and comparisons
- Cancel the existing policy first so the applicant is never covered by two policies at once
- Charge an extra fee
> Replacement rules protect consumers from losing benefits. Unnecessary replacement may be twisting or churning.

[i021] life
Term life insurance provides:
* Temporary protection for a specified period, with no cash value
- Lifetime protection with level premiums and guaranteed cash value growth
- Investment accounts only
- Income for life to the insured, starting once the specified term period ends
> Term is pure protection. If the insured outlives the term, no benefit is paid.

[i022] life
In level term insurance, the:
- Face amount decreases every year to match a loan balance
* Face amount stays the same during the term
- Premium increases every month as the insured grows older
- Policy builds cash value that is paid out at the end of the term
> Level term keeps the same death benefit; premiums are often level for the term as well.

[i023] life
Decreasing term insurance is commonly used to:
- Fund retirement income for the insured after the mortgage is paid off
* Cover a debt that decreases over time, such as a mortgage
- Build cash value that can later be borrowed to pay down a mortgage
- Pay for long-term care expenses that increase as the insured grows older
> The face amount decreases over the term, matching a declining loan balance.

[i024] life
A renewable term policy allows the insured to:
* Renew without evidence of insurability, at a premium based on attained age
- Convert to an annuity
- Renew coverage at the original issue-age premium after passing a new medical exam
- Borrow against the cash value that builds up during each successive renewal period
> Premiums increase at each renewal because the insured is older, but no new medical underwriting is required.

[i025] life
A convertible term policy allows the insured to:
- Increase the face amount every year without any evidence of insurability
* Change to permanent insurance without evidence of insurability
- Receive annual policy dividends that reduce the premium for the rest of the term
- Skip premiums
> Conversion protects insurability: the insured can switch to whole life even after becoming uninsurable.

[i026] life
Whole life insurance provides:
- Coverage for 20 years only, with premiums that increase at each five-year renewal
* Lifetime protection with level premiums and guaranteed cash values
- Flexible premiums and policyowner-directed investment choices in separate accounts
- Coverage only for accidental death, with a double benefit for death on public transit
> Whole life matures (endows) at a stated age such as 100 or 121 and builds guaranteed cash value.

[i027] life
In a limited-pay whole life policy, premiums are paid:
* For a limited period (such as 20 years or to age 65), but coverage lasts for life
- For the insured's whole life, but coverage ends once the insured reaches age 65
- Only once
- Only in the years the policyowner chooses, as long as the cash value covers the policy charges
> Because premiums are compressed into fewer years, they are higher and cash value builds faster.

[i028] life
A single premium whole life policy:
- Requires level premiums for life and builds cash value only after the third policy year
* Is paid with one lump-sum premium and has immediate cash value
- Has no cash value
- Is a type of term insurance that is paid for with a single premium at issue
> A single large premium creates immediate cash value; such policies are often modified endowment contracts.

[i029] life
Universal life insurance is known for:
- Fixed premiums and a fixed death benefit, with cash value guaranteed to equal the face amount at age 65
* Flexible premiums and an adjustable death benefit, with cash value earning current interest
- Cash value invested only in mutual funds chosen by the policyowner, with no interest guarantee
- Having no cash value
> The policyowner can change premium payments and the face amount within limits; there is a guaranteed minimum interest rate.

[i030] life
Under universal life death benefit Option B (Option 2), the death benefit equals:
- The face amount only, which stays level for the life of the policy
* The face amount plus the cash value
- The cash value only
- The face amount plus all premiums paid to date
> Option A is level (face amount); Option B is increasing (face amount plus cash value).

[i031] life
To sell variable life insurance, an agent needs:
- Only a life insurance license, since variable life is regulated solely by the states
* A life insurance license and a securities registration (FINRA)
- A property and casualty license plus a state investment adviser license
- No license
> Variable products invest in separate accounts and are considered securities.

[i032] life
In variable life insurance, the cash value is invested in:
- The insurer's general account, with a guaranteed minimum rate set at issue
* Separate accounts chosen by the policyowner, so values can rise or fall
- Government bonds only, with interest guaranteed by the insurer for the life of the policy
- A savings account
> The policyowner bears the investment risk. The death benefit typically has a guaranteed minimum.

[i033] life
A joint life (first-to-die) policy pays the death benefit:
* When the first of the insureds dies
- Only when both insureds die
- When the youngest insured turns 65
- Only for accidental deaths
> Joint life covers two or more people and pays once, at the first death.

[i034] life
A survivorship (second-to-die) policy is often used for:
- Mortgage protection, paying off the loan at the first death
* Estate tax planning, paying at the second death
- Paying for a child's college costs at the first death
- Disability income for the surviving spouse after the first death
> Survivorship life pays when the last insured dies, often when estate taxes are due.

[i035] life
Modified whole life insurance has:
* Lower premiums for the first few years, then higher level premiums
- Premiums that decrease every year until the policy is paid up at age 65
- No premiums after the first year, because the policy is paid with a single premium
- No cash value
> Modified whole life helps people whose income is expected to grow, such as young professionals.

[i036] life
An accelerated (living) benefit rider allows:
- Premiums to be paid faster so the policy becomes paid up early, like a limited-pay policy
* The insured to receive part of the death benefit early if terminally or chronically ill
- The beneficiary to receive double the face amount if the insured dies as the result of an accident
- The policy to be converted to term insurance without evidence of insurability at any time
> Any amount paid early reduces the death benefit paid to the beneficiary.

[i037] life
A guaranteed insurability rider allows the insured to:
* Buy additional coverage at specified ages or life events without proof of insurability
- Stop paying premiums after becoming totally disabled, following a six-month waiting period
- Receive double the face amount if death results from an accident within 90 days of the injury
- Cover a spouse
> Typical option dates include certain birthdays, marriage or the birth of a child.

[i038] life
Credit life insurance:
- Pays the insured's family
* Pays off the insured's loan balance to the creditor, and cannot exceed the debt
- Builds large cash values that the debtor may borrow against to repay the creditor
- Is always permanent whole life insurance that builds cash value for the insured's family
> Credit life is usually decreasing term, with the creditor as beneficiary.

[i039] life
Most group life insurance is issued as:
- Whole life
* Annually renewable term
- Variable universal life
- Endowment
> Group life is usually yearly renewable term; members often may convert to individual coverage when leaving the group.

[i040] life
In indexed universal life, interest credited to the cash value is:
- Fixed for the life of the policy at the interest rate the insurer declared at issue
* Linked to the performance of a market index, usually with a minimum floor and a cap
- Paid from separate accounts whose values rise and fall directly with the investments
- Never credited
> Indexed products offer some upside linked to an index while protecting against losses through a floor.

[i041] provisions
The entire contract provision states that:
* The policy and a copy of the attached application make up the entire contract
- The agent can add verbal promises later, as long as they are made before the policy is delivered
- The insurer may change the policy terms at any time by mailing a written notice to the owner
- Only the application matters if it conflicts with the policy, since the applicant signed it
> Nothing outside the policy and attached application can be part of the contract, and the insurer cannot change it without the owner's consent.

[i042] provisions
After a life policy has been in force for a certain period (usually 2 years), the insurer cannot contest it for misstatements in the application. This is the:
- Grace period
* Incontestability clause
- Free look provision
- Reinstatement provision
> After the contestable period, the insurer generally cannot void the policy because of misstatements, with limited exceptions.

[i043] provisions
During the grace period of a life insurance policy (commonly 30 or 31 days):
- The policy is canceled and must be reinstated with evidence of insurability before coverage resumes
* Coverage continues; if the insured dies, the unpaid premium is deducted from the benefit
- Coverage is suspended, and no death benefit is paid unless the overdue premium was received
- The premium doubles
> The grace period prevents an unintended lapse for late payment.

[i044] provisions
If the insured's age was misstated on the application, the insurer will usually:
- Void the policy and return the premiums, because age is a material fact in underwriting
* Adjust the benefit to what the premium paid would have bought at the correct age
- Pay double the face amount
- Refund all premiums paid, with interest, and rescind the policy from its issue date
> The misstatement of age provision adjusts benefits instead of canceling the policy.

[i045] provisions
Under a typical suicide clause, if the insured commits suicide within the first two years, the insurer:
- Pays the full death benefit, since suicide is covered from the first policy day
* Refunds the premiums paid instead of paying the death benefit
- Pays half the death benefit and refunds the remaining premiums paid
- Cancels coverage for the primary beneficiary and pays the contingent beneficiary instead
> After the suicide exclusion period, suicide is covered like any other cause of death.

[i046] provisions
To reinstate a lapsed life policy (often within 3 years), the owner usually must:
* Provide evidence of insurability and pay back premiums with interest
- Apply for a new policy at the current attained age, with a new suicide exclusion period
- Pay only the next premium due, with no evidence of insurability required
- Wait 10 years
> Reinstatement restores the original policy; a new contestable period may apply to the reinstatement application.

[i047] provisions
The free look provision allows a new policyowner to:
- Skip the first premium
* Return the policy within a set number of days for a full refund
- Change the beneficiary without notifying the insurer during the first 30 days
- Borrow the full face amount interest-free during the first 10 days
> Free look periods are set by state law and are often 10 days, longer for some products and replacements.

[i048] provisions
If a policyowner dies with an outstanding policy loan, the insurer will:
- Collect the unpaid loan from the beneficiary's own funds before paying the claim
* Deduct the loan balance plus interest from the death benefit
- Cancel the loan
- Refuse to pay the claim until the beneficiary repays the loan balance
> Policy loans are not required to be repaid, but any balance reduces the benefit.

[i049] provisions
The automatic premium loan provision:
* Prevents a lapse by paying an overdue premium with a loan against the cash value
- Increases the face amount automatically each year to keep pace with the cost of living
- Pays premiums when the insured is totally disabled, after a six-month waiting period
- Applies only to term insurance
> It works only if the policy has enough cash value to cover the premium.

[i050] provisions
Which nonforfeiture option provides the same face amount as the original policy for a shorter period of time?
- Cash surrender
- Reduced paid-up
* Extended term
- Interest only
> Extended term uses the cash value to buy term insurance for the full face amount. It is often the automatic option.

[i051] provisions
The reduced paid-up nonforfeiture option provides:
- A lump sum of cash
* A smaller amount of permanent coverage with no further premiums
- The full face amount as term coverage for as long as the cash value lasts
- An annuity for life, starting when the insured reaches the policy's maturity age
> The cash value buys a paid-up whole life policy with a lower face amount.

[i052] provisions
Dividends paid on participating life insurance policies are generally:
- Taxable as ordinary income
* Considered a return of excess premium and not taxable
- Guaranteed every year at the rate stated in the policy at issue
- Paid only on term policies issued by stock insurance companies
> Dividends are not guaranteed. Interest earned on accumulated dividends is taxable.

[i053] provisions
Under the interest only settlement option, the insurer:
* Holds the death proceeds and pays the beneficiary only the interest earned
- Pays the full amount immediately in one lump sum, including all interest earned
- Pays the proceeds plus interest in equal installments over a fixed number of years
- Pays income for the rest of the beneficiary's life, no matter how long the beneficiary lives
> The principal can be paid later, for example to a contingent beneficiary.

[i054] provisions
A settlement option that pays the proceeds plus interest in installments over a specified number of years is:
- Life income
* Fixed period
- Fixed amount
- Lump sum
> With fixed period, the time is set and the payment size is calculated. With fixed amount, the payment size is set.

[i055] provisions
The life income settlement option:
- Pays installments for exactly 10 years, then stops even if the beneficiary is alive
* Guarantees payments for as long as the beneficiary lives
- Pays only the interest earned, while the insurer holds the principal
- Is a lump sum
> Life income works like an annuity for the beneficiary.

[i056] provisions
A contingent beneficiary receives the death benefit:
- Before the primary beneficiary, if the contingent beneficiary is a minor child
* Only if the primary beneficiary has died before the insured
- Along with the primary beneficiary, in equal shares, in all cases
- Only if the insured is still alive when the policy reaches its maturity date
> The contingent (secondary) beneficiary is the backup to the primary beneficiary.

[i057] provisions
An irrevocable beneficiary:
- Can be changed by the policyowner at any time, simply by filing a written change request with the insurer
* Must consent before the owner can change the beneficiary or take actions such as policy loans
- Has no rights at all
- Is always the insured's estate
> An irrevocable beneficiary has a vested interest in the policy.

[i058] provisions
Under the Uniform Simultaneous Death Act, if the insured and the primary beneficiary die at the same time and the order cannot be determined, the proceeds are paid:
* As if the insured survived the primary beneficiary (to the contingent beneficiary or estate)
- To the primary beneficiary's estate, as though the primary beneficiary had survived the insured
- To the insurer
- Equally to all relatives
> This prevents the proceeds from passing through the primary beneficiary's estate.

[i059] provisions
A spendthrift clause protects:
- The insurer from lawsuits filed by beneficiaries over delayed claim payments
* Death proceeds from the beneficiary's creditors before they are paid
- The agent from losing renewal commissions if the policy is later replaced
- The policyowner from having to pay premiums after becoming totally disabled
> It is used with installment settlement options to protect beneficiaries who may mismanage money.

[i060] provisions
The waiver of premium rider:
- Doubles the death benefit if the insured dies in an accident within 90 days of the injury
* Waives premiums if the insured becomes totally disabled, after a waiting period
- Lets the insured buy additional coverage at set ages without proof of insurability
- Pays the insured's medical bills
> A typical waiting period is about six months, and premiums paid during it are often refunded.

[i061] annuities
The main purpose of an annuity is to:
- Create an immediate estate at death for the beneficiaries named by the owner
* Provide a stream of income, especially income that cannot be outlived
- Pay medical expenses not covered by Medicare after the annuitant retires
- Replace life insurance for young families who need a large death benefit at low cost
> Life insurance creates an estate; an annuity systematically liquidates one.

[i062] annuities
During the accumulation period of a deferred annuity:
* Premiums are paid and earnings grow tax-deferred
- Income payments are made to the annuitant
- The annuity cannot earn interest
- Withdrawals are always tax-free
> The annuitization (payout) period begins when the owner starts receiving income.

[i063] annuities
Under current federal law (SECURE 2.0), required minimum distributions from a traditional IRA generally must begin at age:
- 59½
- 65
* 73
- 80
> RMDs generally start at 73 for people who reach age 72 after 2022, rising to 75 later under the same law. Roth IRAs have no RMDs for the original owner.

[i064] annuities
An immediate annuity:
- Is paid for with monthly premiums for 30 years before any income payments can begin
* Is bought with a single premium, and income payments begin within one year
- Never pays income
- Is a type of term life insurance that pays a lump sum if the annuitant dies early
> Immediate annuities are often called SPIAs (single premium immediate annuities).

[i065] annuities
Which annuity payout option pays the highest monthly income for a single annuitant?
* Straight life (life only)
- Life with 10-year period certain
- Joint and survivor
- Cash refund
> Straight life pays only while the annuitant lives, with nothing left to beneficiaries, so it pays the most per month.

[i066] annuities
Under a life with period certain option, if the annuitant dies before the period ends:
- Payments stop immediately, and the remaining balance is kept by the insurer
* The beneficiary receives the remaining guaranteed payments
- The insurer keeps all of the remaining funds as a mortality gain
- The beneficiary receives double payments until the original period ends
> Payments last for life, but are guaranteed for at least the period certain.

[i067] annuities
A joint and survivor annuity:
- Stops all payments when the first of the two annuitants dies
* Continues payments as long as either annuitant is alive
- Pays only one lump sum
- Covers more than ten annuitants, with payments split equally among them
> Payments to the survivor may be the full amount or a reduced percentage, as chosen.

[i068] annuities
In a fixed annuity, the investment risk is carried by:
* The insurance company
- The annuitant
- The agent
- The beneficiary
> Fixed annuities credit a guaranteed minimum rate, and the insurer guarantees the payments.

[i069] annuities
In a variable annuity, the investment risk is carried by:
- The insurance company
* The annuitant (contract owner)
- The state guaranty fund
- The agent
> Variable annuities invest in separate accounts, and values rise or fall with the investments.

[i070] annuities
Surrender charges on an annuity:
- Are charged only after age 65
* Apply to withdrawals or surrender during the early years of the contract
- Are a tax penalty
- Never apply to deferred annuities, only to immediate annuities bought with a single premium
> Surrender charges usually decline each year until they disappear.

[i071] annuities
Withdrawals of earnings from a nonqualified annuity before age 59½ generally face:
- No tax at all
* Ordinary income tax on the earnings plus a 10% penalty, unless an exception applies
- Long-term capital gains tax on the earnings only, with no additional penalty
- A 50% federal penalty on the entire withdrawal, including the owner's original principal
> Exceptions include death and disability. The 10% penalty applies to the taxable portion.

[i072] annuities
Partial withdrawals from a nonqualified deferred annuity are generally taxed:
* As earnings first (taxable), then as a return of principal
- As a return of principal first (tax-free), then as taxable earnings
- Never
- At a flat 15% rate on the full amount withdrawn, regardless of earnings
> This "last-in, first-out" treatment means gains come out first and are taxed as ordinary income.

[i073] annuities
The exclusion ratio determines:
- The surrender charge that applies if the owner cancels during the early contract years
* The portion of each annuity payment that is a tax-free return of principal
- The amount of the death benefit paid to the beneficiary if the annuitant dies early
- The agent's commission on each premium payment made during the accumulation period
> The rest of each payment is interest (earnings) and is taxable.

[i074] annuities
Life insurance death benefits paid to a named beneficiary in a lump sum are generally:
- Taxed as ordinary income
* Not subject to federal income tax
- Taxed as capital gains
- Subject to a 10% penalty
> Interest earned if proceeds are paid in installments is taxable, but the death benefit itself generally is not.

[i075] annuities
Premiums paid by an individual for personal life insurance are:
- Fully tax-deductible
* Not tax-deductible
- Deductible only after age 65
- Deductible as a business expense in all cases
> Because premiums are not deductible, the death benefit is generally received income-tax free.

[i076] annuities
The growth of cash value inside a life insurance policy is:
* Tax-deferred
- Taxed every year
- Taxed at a 50% rate
- Taxed only if the insured dies
> Gains are taxed only if the policy is surrendered for more than the owner's cost basis.

[i077] annuities
A life insurance policy that fails the "7-pay test" becomes a:
- Term policy
* Modified endowment contract (MEC)
- Single premium deferred annuity (SPDA)
- Group policy
> Loans and withdrawals from a MEC are taxed as earnings first and may face a 10% penalty before 59½.

[i078] annuities
A Section 1035 exchange allows:
- A tax-free exchange of an annuity contract for a new cash value life insurance policy
* A tax-free exchange of a life insurance policy for another life policy or an annuity
- Cashing out a policy without tax
- Transferring a policy to a new beneficiary
> Life to life, life to annuity, and annuity to annuity qualify. Annuity to life does not.

[i079] annuities
Qualified distributions from a Roth IRA are:
- Fully taxable
* Tax-free
- Taxed at capital gains rates
- Subject to a 10% penalty
> Roth contributions are made with after-tax dollars, so qualified withdrawals are tax-free.

[i080] annuities
During the accumulation period, a variable annuity owner's account value is measured in:
* Accumulation units
- Annuity units
- Shares of the insurer's stock
- Dividend credits
> During payout, the accumulation units are converted to annuity units.

[i081] health
Disability income insurance is designed to:
- Pay hospital and surgical bills for injuries caused by an accident
* Replace a portion of income lost because of a disability
- Pay for nursing home and home health care costs after an illness
- Provide a death benefit to the family if the insured dies from an accident
> Benefits are usually limited to a percentage of earnings to keep an incentive to return to work.

[i082] health
In disability income insurance, the elimination period is:
* The waiting period after the disability begins before benefits are paid
- The period right after policy issue during which sickness is not covered
- The maximum length of time that disability benefits will be paid for any one disability
- The grace period for premiums
> A longer elimination period lowers the premium. It works like a deductible measured in time.

[i083] health
Which definition of total disability is most favorable to the insured?
- Any occupation
* Own occupation
- Any gainful employment
- Presumptive disability only after 5 years
> Under own occupation, the insured is disabled if they cannot perform the duties of their own job, even if they could do other work.

[i084] health
A residual disability benefit:
- Pays only if the insured is totally disabled and unable to work in any occupation for at least a year
* Pays a partial benefit for lost income when the insured returns to work with reduced earnings
- Pays the full benefit forever
- Is a type of life insurance
> Residual benefits encourage returning to work by making up part of the income loss.

[i085] health
The probationary period in a health policy is:
- The waiting period after a disability begins before income benefits start
* A period after the policy is issued during which sickness is not covered
- The time the agent has to deliver the policy and collect the first premium from the insured
- The free look period during which the policyowner may return the policy for a refund
> Probationary periods discourage people from buying coverage only after they know they are sick. Accidents are usually covered immediately.

[i086] health
An accidental death and dismemberment (AD&D) policy pays the:
* Principal sum for accidental death and a capital sum for losses such as a limb or sight
- Full principal sum for death from any cause, including illness, and half for losing a limb
