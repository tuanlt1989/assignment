# UK construction Health, Safety & Environment (CSCS Operatives) practice bank — English master.
# Original items covering published HS&E test topics. Not official CITB questions.

[s001] general
What is the main purpose of a site induction?
* To explain the site rules, hazards and emergency procedures before you start work
- To check your trade qualifications and test your practical skills before you start work
- To agree your pay rate
- To issue your CSCS card and register your details on the site's access control system
> Everyone must attend the induction before starting work on a new site.

[s002] general
Under health and safety law, employees must:
- Follow safety rules only while a supervisor or site manager is present to check on them
* Take reasonable care of themselves and others and cooperate with their employer
- Buy their own safety equipment, including the hard hats and harnesses the job requires
- Decide their own safety rules
> The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act places duties on both employers and employees.

[s003] general
Under RIDDOR, an injury that keeps a worker off normal duties for more than how many days must be reported to the HSE?
- 1 day
- 3 days
* 7 days
- 30 days
> "Over-seven-day" injuries are reportable (not counting the day of the accident). Over-three-day injuries must still be recorded.

[s004] general
Who is responsible for reporting a reportable accident to the HSE under RIDDOR?
- The injured worker, within 10 days of the accident happening
* The employer or person in control of the site
- The hospital that treated the injury, as part of its patient records
- The local council
> Workers should report all accidents to their supervisor so the responsible person can report them.

[s005] general
Which incidents should be written in the site accident book?
- Only accidents that need hospital treatment or time off work
* All accidents and injuries, however minor
- Only accidents caused by plant
- Only accidents to visitors
> Recording every injury helps prevent repeats and protects the injured person's rights.

[s006] general
A near miss is:
- An accident that caused a minor injury treated with the site first-aid kit
* An event that could have caused injury or damage but did not
- A late delivery
- Missing a toolbox talk without telling your supervisor beforehand
> Reporting near misses helps fix hazards before someone gets hurt.

[s007] general
A method statement describes:
- Your hourly rate
* How a particular task will be carried out safely, step by step
- The site's working hours and the times when deliveries are allowed
- A summary of the company's accident statistics over the last five years
> Read and follow the method statement for your task. If the job changes, stop and ask.

[s008] general
The purpose of a risk assessment is to:
* Identify hazards and decide how to control the risks
- Record who was to blame after an accident so insurers can be told
- Replace training
- Calculate the cost of the job, including the price of any safety equipment
> Control measures from the risk assessment must be followed by everyone doing the work.

[s009] general
In the hierarchy of control, what should be considered first?
- Issuing personal protective equipment to everyone
- Putting up warning signs around the hazard
* Eliminating the hazard altogether
- Training
> Eliminate, substitute, engineering controls, administrative controls, and PPE as the last resort.

[s010] general
You see a colleague working in an unsafe way. You should:
- Ignore it because it is not your job
* Speak up or stop the work if safe to do so, and report it to your supervisor
- Take a photo of it and post it in the site group chat so everyone can see what went wrong
- Wait and mention it at the next weekly toolbox talk so the whole team hears about it
> Everyone has a duty to look out for others. Most sites encourage you to stop unsafe work.

[s011] general
You are taking prescribed medicine that makes you drowsy. You should:
- Keep working as normal
* Tell your supervisor before you start work
- Drink plenty of strong coffee so that you stay alert when operating plant
- Stop taking the medicine for the day without asking your doctor
> Medicines, drugs and alcohol can affect your ability to work safely.

[s012] general
Which welfare facilities must be provided on a construction site?
- A gym and canteen
* Toilets, washing facilities, drinking water and a rest area
- A parking space for every worker and a canteen serving hot meals
- Showers and a drying room, but only for managers and site visitors
> Facilities for changing and drying clothes are also needed where appropriate.

[s013] general
A safety sign with a red circle and a diagonal red line means:
* You must not do something (prohibition)
- You must do something (mandatory)
- Warning of a hazard
- A safe condition, such as a first-aid point
> Example: "No smoking."

[s014] general
A blue circular safety sign means:
- Prohibition
* You must do something (mandatory)
- Warning of a hazard nearby, such as a trip hazard
- The location of fire-fighting equipment
> Example: "Hard hats must be worn."

[s015] general
A yellow triangular sign with a black border means:
- Fire exit or escape route
- A mandatory action you must carry out
* Warning of a hazard
- Drinking water
> Example: "Danger: overhead cables."

[s016] general
A green rectangular or square sign shows:
- A prohibition, such as no entry for anyone who is not authorised
- A warning
* A safe condition, such as a first-aid point or emergency exit
- The location of fire-fighting equipment such as extinguishers and hose reels
> Red rectangular signs show fire-fighting equipment.

[s017] general
Someone is injured on site and you are not a trained first aider. You should:
- Help them into a vehicle and drive them straight to the nearest hospital A&E department
* Call for the site first aider or emergency services and follow instructions
- Give them painkillers and a hot sweet drink, then let them rest in the cabin
- Leave them and carry on working
> Do not try treatment beyond your training. Make the area safe if you can.

[s018] general
When the fire alarm sounds you should:
* Stop work, go to the assembly point by the safest route and wait for the roll call
- Finish the task you are on first, then leave by the route you normally use to the canteen
- Collect your tools and move your van away from the building before going to the assembly point
- Go home
> Never go back into the building until you are told it is safe.

[s019] general
Using a mobile phone while walking through a busy area of site is dangerous because:
- It drains the battery
* It distracts you from hazards such as moving plant and trip hazards
- It is a criminal offence to use a phone anywhere on a construction site
- Its radio signal can interfere with tower crane controls and site radios
> Stop in a safe place before using your phone.

[s020] general
A toolbox talk is:
- A session where damaged hand tools are collected and repaired on site
* A short safety briefing on a specific topic, usually given on site
- A formal written test that must be passed before you can get a CSCS card
- A union meeting
> Toolbox talks keep the team informed about current risks.

[s021] health
Cutting concrete or stone can create silica dust. The best way to control it is:
- Work quickly and stand upwind so that the dust is blown away from your face
* Water suppression or on-tool extraction, plus suitable face-fitted RPE
- Wear a disposable paper dust mask, which seals well enough over a short beard
- Cut only in the afternoon
> Respirable crystalline silica can cause silicosis and lung cancer.

[s022] health
Why must tight-fitting dust masks be face-fit tested?
- To check the mask's colour code matches the type of dust on site
* To make sure the mask seals properly on your face
- To meet a fashion rule
- To record the mask's serial number in the site's PPE register
> Facial hair under the seal stops a tight-fitting mask from working. Be clean-shaven or use powered RPE with a hood.

[s023] health
If you have to shout to be heard by someone about 2 metres away, the noise level is probably:
- Completely safe, as hearing is only damaged by sudden bangs
* High enough that hearing protection is likely needed
- Lower than normal speech
- Only a problem if you are exposed to it for more than 12 hours a day
> Noise damage builds up over time and is permanent.

[s024] health
At what daily noise exposure level must employers provide hearing protection and enforce hearing protection zones?
- 70 dB(A)
- 75 dB(A)
- 80 dB(A)
* 85 dB(A)
> At 80 dB(A) (the lower action value) protection must be made available; at 85 dB(A) (the upper action value) it must be worn.

[s025] health
Early symptoms of hand-arm vibration syndrome include:
* Tingling, numbness and fingers turning white
- Headaches, sneezing and a runny nose after using the tool
- Back pain and stiffness only, mostly first thing in the morning
- Itchy eyes
> HAVS is caused by regular use of vibrating tools such as breakers and grinders.

[s026] health
Which action helps reduce the risk of hand-arm vibration?
- Gripping the tool as tightly as possible so that it cannot kick back or slip
* Using low-vibration tools, limiting trigger time and keeping hands warm
- Using worn or blunt bits so you have to press the tool harder against the work
- Working through breaks
> Report symptoms early. Health surveillance may be provided.

[s027] health
Leptospirosis (Weil's disease) is mainly caught from:
- Skin contact with wet cement or lime mortar
* Water or soil contaminated by rat urine
- Asbestos
- Breathing diesel fumes from plant in poorly ventilated areas
> Cover cuts, wear gloves, and wash your hands before eating, drinking or smoking.

[s028] health
Contact dermatitis on site is often caused by:
- Working outdoors in strong sunlight without wearing any sunscreen
* Wet cement, solvents and other chemicals on the skin
- Exposure to loud noise from breakers, saws and other plant
- Vibration
> Wear gloves, wash skin, use moisturiser and report rashes early.

[s029] health
Wet cement left on the skin can cause:
- Only mild dryness, as cement is harmless once it has been mixed with water
* Serious chemical burns because it is strongly alkaline
- Leptospirosis, from bacteria that grow in the wet cement mix
- Vibration white finger
> Wash it off immediately with clean water. Kneeling in wet concrete can cause deep burns.

[s030] health
COSHH stands for:
* Control of Substances Hazardous to Health
- Control of Safety at Heights and Hoisting operations
- Control of Site Hazards and Hygiene
- Code of Safe Handling Heavy loads
> COSHH assessments explain the hazards of a substance and the controls you must use.

[s031] health
You think you have uncovered asbestos while working. You should:
- Break off a small piece and take it to the site office so it can be checked
* Stop work immediately, keep others away and tell your supervisor
- Dampen it down, then sweep it up and put it in the general waste skip
- Spray it with paint
> Asbestos fibres can cause mesothelioma, asbestosis and lung cancer, often decades later.

[s032] health
Diseases caused by asbestos usually appear:
- Within a few hours of breathing in the fibres
- Within a week or two of the exposure
* Many years after exposure
- Only if you smoke
> There is no cure, so prevention is essential.

[s033] health
Before lifting a heavy load by hand, you should first consider:
* Whether the load can be moved with a lifting aid or mechanical equipment
- How quickly you can lift it, so that the weight spends less time in your hands
- Lifting it with a twisted back
- Whether a workmate can throw it up to you to save carrying it up the ladder
> Avoid manual handling where possible; otherwise assess the load, the task and your capability.

[s034] health
The correct technique for lifting a load by hand includes:
- Keeping your legs straight and bending from the waist so your back takes the weight
* Bending your knees, keeping your back straight and the load close to your body
- Holding the load out at arm's length so that it does not dirty or snag your clothing
- Twisting while lifting
> Plan the route, get a good grip and avoid twisting.

[s035] health
Working outdoors in strong sun without protection increases the risk of:
- Silicosis from breathing in hot, dusty air
* Skin cancer and heat stress
- Leptospirosis, carried by insects in hot weather
- Hearing loss
> Cover up, use sunscreen, drink water and take breaks in the shade.

[s036] health
The purpose of health surveillance, such as hearing tests, is to:
- Decide which workers are entitled to a pay rise for good attendance
* Detect early signs of work-related ill health
- Replace PPE
- Prove you are fit enough to stop wearing hearing protection
> Early detection allows action before the damage gets worse.

[s037] health
You are feeling very tired and unable to concentrate on a high-risk task. You should:
- Keep going to finish on time
* Tell your supervisor and take a break
- Drink an energy drink and carry on
- Swap to operating plant instead, as it needs less effort
> Fatigue increases the risk of accidents.

[s038] health
If you are struggling with stress or poor mental health at work, a good step is to:
* Talk to your supervisor, a mental health first aider or your GP
- Keep it to yourself until your annual review, then raise it with the HR department
- Use alcohol to relax at lunch
- Leave site without telling anyone and come back when you feel better
> Many sites have trained mental health first aiders and support lines.

[s039] health
Drinking water on site should be:
- Taken from any tap including those marked "not for drinking"
* Clearly marked and readily available
- Only available in the office
- Stored in old chemical containers
> Never store drinks in containers that held chemicals.

[s040] health
Which is the safest way to deal with a hazardous substance you have not used before?
- Smell it to identify it
* Read the COSHH assessment or safety data sheet and follow the controls
- Assume it is safe if it has no smell and use it as you would any similar product
- Ask a colleague to try it first and tell you if it causes any irritation
> If there is no information, do not use it. Ask your supervisor.

[s041] safety
Who must pay for the PPE you need to do your job safely?
* Your employer
- You, through a deduction from your wages
- The client, as part of the contract
- The HSE
> PPE required for the job must be provided free of charge, and you must use and look after it.

[s042] safety
Your hard hat has a crack in the shell. You should:
- Seal the crack with strong tape and keep using it
* Stop using it and get a replacement
- Paint over the crack
- Keep using it until your next PPE check, then report it
> Also do not paint or add unapproved stickers, as some chemicals weaken the shell.

[s043] safety
High-visibility clothing is worn so that:
- You look professional
* You can be seen by plant and vehicle operators
- It keeps you warm and dry in cold or wet weather
- Supervisors can easily spot you when they need you for overtime work
> Keep hi-vis clean and fastened.

[s044] safety
When should eye protection be worn?
* When there is a risk from flying particles, dust, chemicals or sparks
- Only on sunny days
- Only when working at night or in poor light, when it is harder to see hazards
- Not needed if you already wear prescription glasses with plastic lenses
> Use the type of eye protection stated in the risk assessment.

[s045] safety
Most UK sites use yellow 110V portable power tools because:
- They are more powerful than 230V tools, so that jobs can be finished faster
* Reduced voltage greatly lowers the risk of a fatal electric shock
- They are cheaper
- They do not need regular inspection or PAT testing like 230V tools do
> 110V centre-tapped systems limit the voltage to earth to about 55V.

[s046] safety
You notice a damaged cable on a power tool. You should:
- Wrap the damaged part with insulation tape and keep working carefully
* Stop using it, label it as faulty and report it
- Use it outdoors only, where the ground is dry, until it can be repaired
- Give it to a colleague
> Check tools before each use and use only tested equipment.

[s047] safety
Before digging, buried services should be located by:
- Working out where services run from the position of street lights and manhole covers
* Checking service drawings and using a cable avoidance tool (CAT and Genny)
- Digging quickly with a machine
- Asking the client or the neighbours where they think the buried pipes and cables run
> Then hand dig carefully near the services that were found.

[s048] safety
When working near overhead power lines, you should:
- Assume the lines are dead unless there is a warning sign fixed to the nearest pole
* Keep plant and materials well clear and follow the permit and barrier controls
- Use metal ladders underneath them, as long as you wear rubber-soled boots and gloves
- Touch them to test
> Contact or arcing can be fatal. Goal posts and barriers may be used to mark safe zones.

[s049] safety
The three elements needed for a fire are:
- Water, fuel and wind
* Heat, fuel and oxygen
- Smoke, heat and light
- Flammable gas, sparks and running water
> Remove any one side of the fire triangle to put out a fire.

[s050] safety
Which fire extinguisher is suitable for fires in electrical equipment?
- Water (red label)
* Carbon dioxide (CO2, black label)
- Foam (cream label), sprayed onto the live equipment
- Wet chemical (yellow label)
> Never use water on live electrical equipment.

[s051] safety
Hot work such as welding or cutting usually requires:
* A hot work permit and a fire watch after the work has finished
- No special controls, provided the work is done outdoors in the open air
- A single water extinguisher kept somewhere within 50 metres of the work
- Working alone
> Fires can start long after the work ends, so the area is checked for a set period afterward.

[s052] safety
Ladders on construction sites should be used:
- For any job lasting up to a full working day, as long as someone foots the ladder
* Only for short, light work where a safer option is not reasonably practicable
- For carrying heavy materials up and down, provided you keep one hand free at all times
- Without anyone checking them
> Keep three points of contact and never overreach.

[s053] safety
The correct angle for a leaning ladder is about:
- 1 out for every 1 up (about 45 degrees)
* 1 out for every 4 up (about 75 degrees)
- 1 out for every 10 up (about 84 degrees)
- Vertical against the wall
> This is known as the 1-in-4 rule.

[s054] safety
A ladder used to access a working platform should extend above the landing point by about:
- 10 cm
* 1 metre (about three rungs)
- 3 metres
- It does not need to extend if the ladder is tied
> This gives a secure handhold when stepping on and off.

[s055] safety
Who is allowed to alter a scaffold?
- Any trade that needs better access, if they put it back afterwards
* Only a competent, trained scaffolder
- The site manager, if the client has agreed to the change
- The client
> Never remove guardrails, ties or boards. Check the scaffold inspection tag before use.

[s056] safety
The minimum height of the top guardrail on a working platform is:
- 500 mm
- 750 mm
* 950 mm
- 1,500 mm
> Edge protection also needs an intermediate guardrail and a toe board.

[s057] safety
A full-body harness used for fall arrest must be:
- Worn loosely for comfort, with the leg straps left undone when you are not near an edge
* Attached to a suitable anchor point, used by a trained person, with a rescue plan
- Used without inspection
- Clipped to the nearest handrail or scaffold tube, with the lanyard kept as long as possible
> Check harnesses and lanyards before every use.

[s058] safety
Before using a mobile tower scaffold, you should make sure that:
- Workers stay on the top platform while it is pushed, to save climbing down
* Outriggers or stabilisers are fitted and the wheel brakes are on
- It is built as high as possible
- Some platform boards are left out to keep the tower light enough to move
> Only move a tower when nobody is on it and the route is clear.

[s059] safety
A scaffold should be inspected by a competent person:
- Once when it is first erected, and then every three months for as long as it stays up
* Before first use, at least every 7 days, and after anything affecting its safety
- Only after an accident
- Never once it is up
> Look for a current inspection tag or record.

[s060] safety
Falling materials from height can be prevented by:
* Toe boards, brick guards, netting and keeping platforms tidy
- Working faster
- Throwing waste down carefully after shouting a warning to people below
- Wearing ear defenders so that you can concentrate on the work in hand
> Never throw materials from height. Use chutes or lower them safely.

[s061] worksite
When walking around site, you should:
- Take the shortest route across the plant area
* Use designated pedestrian routes
- Walk behind reversing vehicles
- Stand near the excavator bucket to watch
> Segregating people from vehicles is one of the best ways to prevent being struck.

[s062] worksite
Before approaching operating plant, you should:
- Shout loudly and walk up from behind so you do not distract the operator from the work
* Make eye contact with the operator and get a clear signal that it is safe
- Climb onto the machine's step so the operator can hear you over the engine
- Wait in the blind spot
> Operators often cannot see people close to the machine.

[s063] worksite
Reversing vehicles on site should be guided by:
- Any person nearby who can see behind the vehicle
* A trained banksman or vehicle marshal
- The driver alone in all cases, relying on the mirrors
- A sign only
> Reversing should be avoided where possible, for example with one-way systems.

[s064] worksite
Who may operate plant such as excavators or telehandlers?
* Only trained, competent and authorised operators
- Anyone with a full car driving licence and a week's experience
- Anyone over 18
- A labourer who has watched the operator, while the operator is on a break
> Operators usually hold a recognised card such as CPCS or NPORS.

[s065] worksite
Riding on plant is allowed only when:
- The ride is short and you hold on to the cab or a handrail
* There is a proper seat provided for a passenger
- The bucket is empty
- The supervisor has given permission for a short trip
> Falls from plant cause serious injuries.

[s066] worksite
Why should spoil and materials be kept back from the edge of an excavation?
- To keep the site looking tidy for the client and any visitors who come on site
* To reduce the risk of the sides collapsing and things falling in
- To keep them dry
- To leave room for dumpers to drive right up to the edge to tip into it
> Also use edge protection to stop people and vehicles falling in.

[s067] worksite
Excavations must be inspected by a competent person:
- Once a week, plus a check whenever someone reports that the sides look unsafe
* At the start of each shift and after any event that could affect stability
- Only when it rains
- Only once, at the end of the job, before the excavation is backfilled and compacted
> Sides must be supported or battered back to prevent collapse.

[s068] worksite
Why must a petrol generator never be used inside an excavation or enclosed area?
- It is too noisy
* Exhaust gases such as carbon monoxide can build up and kill
- It will overheat without a breeze to cool it, which voids the warranty
- It is too heavy
> Carbon monoxide has no colour or smell.

[s069] worksite
Who may enter a confined space such as a manhole or tank?
- Anyone, if they are quick
* Only trained people with a permit, gas monitoring and a rescue plan in place
- The first person available, as long as they are wearing a harness and a hard hat
- Anyone wearing a dust mask, as long as a colleague waits at the entrance to call for help
> Confined spaces can contain toxic gases or too little oxygen.

[s070] worksite
A colleague has collapsed inside a confined space. You should:
* Raise the alarm and not enter unless you are trained and equipped for rescue
- Go in straight away and pull them out before the fumes or lack of air do more harm
- Take a deep breath, hold it and go in quickly to drag them out into the fresh air
- Wait for them to wake up
> Many deaths in confined spaces are untrained would-be rescuers.

[s071] worksite
During a crane lift, you should:
- Walk under the load if you are quick
* Stay clear of the load and follow the slinger/signaller's instructions
- Steady the load with your hands while it is overhead, to stop it swinging
- Give signals to the crane operator yourself whenever the slinger/signaller is busy
> Only the appointed slinger/signaller gives signals to the crane operator.

[s072] worksite
Before using a lifting accessory such as a sling, you should check that it:
- Is the cheapest available
* Shows its safe working load, has an in-date inspection record and is undamaged
- Is long enough to be wrapped twice around the crane hook so it cannot slip off
- Has been painted
> Damaged or unmarked accessories must not be used.

[s073] worksite
An exclusion zone around work such as lifting or demolition means:
- Only managers, and visitors escorted by site staff, may enter
* Nobody may enter unless authorised for that work
- It is a designated smoking area, away from the main work
- It is a rest area
> Respect barriers and signs.

[s074] worksite
Before working on machinery or electrical systems, isolation should be done by:
- Asking a colleague to stand by the switch and stop anyone turning it back on
* Locking off and tagging the energy source (lock-out/tag-out)
- Unplugging the supply and leaving the plug on the floor beside the machine
- Switching off at the tool only
> Only the person who applied the lock should remove it.

[s075] worksite
Abrasive wheels on grinders should be changed:
- By any operative who has the correct spanner and has read the label on the guard
* Only by trained and competent people, checking the wheel's speed rating
- Only when the wheel has broken
- With the grinder still plugged in
> A wheel running faster than its rating can shatter.

[s076] worksite
When driving a site dumper, you should:
- Carry a passenger in the skip, as long as they sit down and hold on tightly
* Wear the seat belt if a roll-over protective structure is fitted
- Drive with the skip raised
- Drive close along the edge of excavations so the load can be tipped straight in
> Most dumper deaths involve overturning.

[s077] worksite
When working next to or over water, you should:
- Work alone, so that fewer people are at risk of falling into the water
* Wear a lifejacket and make sure rescue equipment is available
- Remove your hi-vis
- Take off your lifejacket in hot weather so that you do not overheat
> Drowning can happen quickly, even in shallow water.

[s078] worksite
When working on roads next to live traffic, you should:
* Stay within the coned safety zone and wear high-visibility clothing
- Move cones into the live lane when they are in your way, then put them back later
- Step into the road to save time
- Work without lights at night
> Traffic management must be set up by trained people.

[s079] worksite
Cartridge-operated (nail gun) tools should only be used by:
- Anyone on site who has used a similar tool before, even without formal training
* Trained and authorised people following the manufacturer's instructions
- Apprentices on their own, once they have watched a demonstration of the tool
- Visitors
> These tools can fire nails through materials and cause fatal injuries.

[s080] worksite
Before starting work in a new area, why should you check the permit-to-work system?
- To book holiday
* Some high-risk work can only start once a permit is issued and its controls are in place
- To find out the canteen menu
- Because every task of any kind needs its own signed permit before it can start in a new area
> Permits are used for confined spaces, hot work, live electrical work and more.

[s081] environment
Fuel or oil is spilt on site. You should:
- Hose it into the nearest drain with plenty of water so that it is diluted quickly
* Contain it with a spill kit, stop it reaching drains or water, and report it
- Cover it with soil, leave it to soak in and tell your supervisor at the end of the day
- Set it on fire
> Pollution incidents may need to be reported to the environmental regulator.

[s082] environment
Surface water drains on site usually lead to:
- A treatment plant that removes all pollution
* Streams and rivers
- A skip
- The site office
> Never pour concrete washout, paint or chemicals down drains.

[s083] environment
Why is waste segregated into different skips on site?
* To allow more waste to be recycled and reduce what goes to landfill
- So that each skip is filled to its maximum weight before it is collected
- To confuse thieves
- Because the law requires a separate skip for every trade working on site
> Put waste into the correct, labelled skip.

[s084] environment
Hazardous waste such as asbestos, oils and some paints must be:
- Mixed with general waste so it is diluted to a safe level
* Kept separate and removed by a licensed carrier
- Burned on site in a metal drum to reduce its volume
- Buried on site
> Ask your supervisor if you are unsure how to dispose of something.

[s085] environment
In the waste hierarchy, what is the most preferred option?
- Disposal to landfill (burying waste at a licensed site)
- Recycling (turning waste into new products or materials)
* Prevention (reducing waste in the first place)
- Burning
> Then reuse, recycle, recover and, last, dispose.

[s086] environment
Dust from site is blowing onto nearby homes. A good control is to:
- Work faster
* Damp down surfaces and cover stockpiles
- Ignore it, because members of the public are not on site
- Use a leaf blower to clear the dust off the paths
> Dust can affect neighbours' health and lead to complaints.

[s087] environment
To reduce noise nuisance to neighbours you should:
- Start noisy work early in the morning so it is finished before people get home
* Work within permitted hours and switch off plant when not in use
- Play site radios loudly so that the machinery noise is less noticeable next door
- Remove silencers from plant
> Local authorities may set working hour limits.

[s088] environment
You find a bird's nest with eggs in a hedge you were told to remove. You should:
- Remove it carefully, put it on the ground nearby and carry on
* Stop work in that area and tell your supervisor
- Move the nest to another hedge nearby and carry on with the job
- Ignore it
> Wild birds' nests are protected by law while in use.

[s089] environment
Japanese knotweed found on site should be:
- Cut down to ground level and put in the general skip along with other green waste
* Left undisturbed and reported, as it is an invasive plant controlled by law
- Spread around the site
- Sprayed with general weedkiller and dug out straight away by the groundworkers
> Even small pieces can regrow. Specialist removal is required.

Why should excavations left open overnight have a way for animals to get out, such as a sloping plank?
- So that workers can climb out quickly
- To support the sides of the excavation and stop them collapsing overnight
- To keep rainwater out
* Because animals such as badgers and hedgehogs can fall in and become trapped
> Check excavations for trapped animals before starting work each day.

A member of the public complains to you about dust coming from the site. What should you do?
- Tell them it is not your problem and that they should write to the council
- Give them your personal phone number
* Be polite and pass the complaint on to your supervisor or the site manager
- Argue with them
> Complaints help the site find and fix problems such as dust, noise and mud on the road.

Work with liquids or fine powders is planned next to a surface water drain. How can the drain be protected?
- Put a cone next to it so that people know not to pour anything into it
* Cover it with a drain cover or mat and keep a spill kit close by
- Pour some water down it first
- Take the grating off
> Anything that gets into a surface water drain can reach a river untreated.

[s250] environment
Empty containers that held hazardous substances should be:
- Rinsed out with water and then used for carrying drinking water to the work area
- Put in the general waste skip
- Burned on site so that any harmful residue is destroyed
* Disposed of as instructed, often as hazardous waste, and never reused for food or drink
> Residues left inside can still be harmful or flammable. Follow the safety data sheet.
