# German security-guard competence exam (Sachkundeprüfung §34a GewO) — English helper/master.
# Original practice items. The IHK exam is in German; de.txt is the primary text in the app.

[k001] oeffrecht
Who grants the permit to run a security business under §34a of the German Trade Regulation Act (GewO)?
- The IHK, after the applicant has passed the competence exam
* The competent authority, usually the local trade office
- The state police headquarters, after a reliability check
- The employer's association for the security industry (BDSW)
> The IHK conducts the competence exam; the trade authority grants the business permit.

[k002] oeffrecht
Who conducts the §34a competence exam (Sachkundeprüfung)?
- The local court
- The police academy
* The Chamber of Industry and Commerce (IHK)
- The security company itself
> The exam has a written part and an oral part.

[k003] oeffrecht
Which activity requires the full competence exam, not just the shorter instruction course (Unterrichtung)?
- Reception service in an office building
* Door control at discotheques and similar venues
- Night watch in a closed warehouse
- Key holding without patrols
> Patrols in public spaces, store detectives, door staff and guarding of refugee accommodation require the competence exam.

* The central security register (Bewacherregister)
- The local population register (Melderegister) only

[k005] oeffrecht
Who is generally considered unreliable under §34a GewO?
* A person convicted with final effect in the last five years of a crime or of certain offences such as theft or assault
- Anyone with a parking fine
- Anyone who changed employers
- Anyone without a driving licence
> Membership of banned or extremist organisations can also make a person unreliable.

[k006] oeffrecht

- Wear police-style uniforms
* Carry a service ID card and show it on request
- Carry a firearm
- Keep their name secret at all times
> The Security Regulation (BewachV) sets rules for ID cards and clothing.

[k007] oeffrecht
Uniforms of security staff must:
* Not be confusable with uniforms of the police or armed forces
- Look exactly like police uniforms so that they command respect
- Always be black
- Be approved in advance by the IHK that conducted the competence exam
> Confusion with state uniforms is not allowed.

[k008] oeffrecht
The state monopoly on the use of force means that:
- Security staff may use force as police do once the client has transferred its house rights to them
* Only the state may use force as a sovereign power; private staff only have rights everyone has
- Security staff may arrest anyone at any time, as long as they hand the person over to the police
- Companies may set their own penal rules
> Private security works with "everyone's rights" (Jedermannsrechte) and rights transferred by the client, such as house rights.

[k009] oeffrecht
"Jedermannsrechte" are rights that:
- Only police officers and other public officials have as part of their sovereign powers
* Every person has, such as self-defence, necessity and provisional arrest
- Only security companies have, once the trade office has granted the §34a permit
- Only property owners have
> Security staff have no more powers than other citizens unless rights are transferred to them, such as house rights.

[k010] oeffrecht
A security business must take out:
- Car insurance only

- Life insurance for every customer
- No insurance

[k011] oeffrecht
Under the GDPR, the principle of data minimisation means:
* Only the personal data needed for the purpose may be processed
- All available data should be collected in case it becomes useful for a later purpose
- Data may be shared freely
- Data must be stored forever so that it is available for later investigations
> Other principles include purpose limitation, accuracy, storage limitation and integrity.

[k012] oeffrecht
Video surveillance of publicly accessible areas:

[k021] bgb
According to §227 BGB, an act required in self-defence is:
* Not unlawful
- Always punishable
- Only allowed for police
- Allowed only against children
> Self-defence is the defence needed to avert a present unlawful attack on oneself or another.

[k022] bgb
Under §228 BGB (defensive necessity), a person may damage or destroy:
- Any object nearby
* An object from which the danger comes, such as an attacking dog, if necessary and proportionate
- Only objects they own themselves, never another person's animal or property, even if it is the source of the danger
- Public property only
> The damage must not be out of proportion to the danger.

[k023] bgb
§904 BGB (aggressive necessity) allows using another person's property to avert a danger. The owner:
- Has no rights
* Can claim compensation for the damage
- Must pay a fine
- Loses ownership
> Example: using someone else's fire extinguisher to fight a fire.

[k024] bgb
Self-help under §229 BGB is allowed when:
- It is quicker than calling the police
* Official help cannot be obtained in time and the claim would otherwise be frustrated or made much more difficult
- The guard wants to punish someone
- The person is a known offender
> Self-help must not go further than needed (§230 BGB).

[k025] bgb
§859 (1) BGB allows the possessor to:
* Defend themselves with force against unlawful interference with possession
- Punish the offender
- Search the bags of anyone on the premises whom they suspect of having stolen something
- Detain anyone found on the premises for up to 24 hours until the owner arrives
> This is called defence of possession (Besitzwehr).

[k026] bgb
Under §859 (2) BGB, a possessor may take back a movable item:
- At any time later, as long as they can prove that they own it
* From an offender caught in the act or pursued immediately
- Only with a court order obtained by the owner of the item beforehand
- Only with police present and after the theft has been reported
> This is called recovery of possession (Besitzkehr).

[k027] bgb
In German civil law, possession means:
- Legal ownership of an item or a property
* Actual control over an item
- A right registered with a notary or in the land register
- A bank account with assets held in one's name
> Ownership is the legal right to an item; possession is the actual physical control.

[k028] bgb
A security guard who controls items for the client (as a "Besitzdiener"):
- Has no right to protect them, as he is neither owner nor possessor
* May exercise the possessor's rights under §859 BGB (§860 BGB)
- Becomes the owner
- Must first obtain a court order before protecting them against anyone
> The guard acts on behalf of the possessor.

[k029] bgb
Under §823 BGB, a person who intentionally or negligently injures another's life, body, health, freedom or property unlawfully must:
- Pay a state fine only
* Compensate the damage caused
- Leave the country
- Do nothing
> This is the central rule of tort liability.

[k030] bgb
Under §831 BGB, an employer may be liable for damage caused by an employee while carrying out work, unless:
* The employer can prove that the employee was carefully selected and supervised
- The employee was on time for the shift and had worked for the company for more than a year
- The damage was below 500 euros and was caused during the employee's probationary period
- The client agrees
> This is liability for vicarious agents (Verrichtungsgehilfen).

- Acting with intent, i.e. knowingly and willingly causing harm

[k036] bgb

[k041] straf
According to §32 StGB, self-defence is:
* The defence required to avert a present unlawful attack from oneself or another
- Any revenge for an earlier attack
- Using force to collect a debt that a court has already confirmed but that the debtor still refuses to pay
- Punishing a thief later
> The defence must be necessary: the mildest effective means.

[k042] straf
Defending another person against an attack is called:
- Self-help (Selbsthilfe) under §229 BGB on behalf of a third party
* Defence of others (Nothilfe), covered by §32 StGB
- Justifying necessity (rechtfertigender Notstand, §34 StGB)
- Provisional arrest (vorläufige Festnahme) under §127 StPO
> The same conditions as self-defence apply.

[k043] straf
Under §33 StGB, a person who exceeds the limits of self-defence out of confusion, fear or fright:
- Is always punished more severely
* Is not punished
- Loses all rights
- Must pay double compensation
> This is called excessive self-defence.

[k044] straf
Justifying necessity under §34 StGB requires that:
- Any interest may be sacrificed, provided the person acting was in danger themselves and did not cause that danger
* The protected interest substantially outweighs the interest that is harmed, and the act is an appropriate means
- The danger is in the past
- The person acts for money
> Example: breaking a window to rescue someone from a fire.

[k045] straf
Under §127 (1) StPO, anyone may provisionally arrest a person who is caught in the act or being pursued if:
- The person is rude
* The person is suspected of fleeing or their identity cannot be established immediately
- The person refuses to buy something
- The person is a foreign national, even if their identity and address have already been established
> The arrest must be proportionate.

[k046] straf
After a provisional arrest, the guard must:
* Inform the police without delay and hand the person over
- Release the person after one day
- Question the person for hours
- Take the person home
> The guard must not punish or interrogate the person.

[k047] straf
A provisional arrest under §127 StPO is allowed for:
- Administrative offences such as parking violations
* Criminal offences (Straftaten)
- Rude behaviour only
- Any rule violation in the house rules
> It does not apply to administrative offences (Ordnungswidrigkeiten).

[k048] straf

[k049] straf
Trespass (§123 StGB) is committed by someone who:
* Unlawfully enters protected premises or stays despite being asked to leave
- Walks along a public street directly in front of a private house late at night
- Enters a shop during normal opening hours without intending to buy anything there
- Calls the police
> Trespass is generally only prosecuted on complaint (Antragsdelikt).

[k050] straf
Theft (§242 StGB) is the taking of:
- One's own property
* A movable item belonging to another, with the intention of unlawfully appropriating it
- Real estate, for example by occupying another person's plot of land without their consent
- Information only
> The attempt is punishable.

[k051] straf
Robbery (§249 StGB) is:
- Any theft committed at night or by several people acting together, even without force or threats
* Theft with violence against a person or threats of present danger to life or limb
- Damage to property
- Fraud
> Robbery is a felony (Verbrechen).

[k052] straf
Robbery-like theft (§252 StGB) is committed by a thief who:
* Is caught in the act and uses violence or threats to keep the stolen goods
- Steals at night
- Steals from a relative or household member and later refuses to return the goods voluntarily
- Returns the goods
> The thief is punished like a robber.

[k053] straf
Coercion (§240 StGB) means:
- Asking someone politely
* Unlawfully forcing someone to act, tolerate or omit something by force or threat of serious harm
- Arresting someone legally
- Refusing to serve a customer or to let a guest in without stating a reason, even when no force or threat is used
> Unlawfully forcing customers to do things can be coercion.

[k054] straf
Locking a person in a room without legal grounds can be:

[k055] straf

- Gives first aid incorrectly despite trying their best, for example by using the wrong position
- Waits for professional help after calling 112
> Calling for help can be sufficient assistance.
