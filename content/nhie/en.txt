# National Home Inspector Examination (NHIE) practice bank.
# Original questions written for this app. Code values are IRC 2021 model-code values;
# local adoption and amendments vary, so always follow the jurisdiction's adopted code.

# ---------------- Site & Exterior (h001-h050) ----------------

[h001] site
Per the IRC, how much should the finished grade fall away from a foundation within the first 10 feet?
- At least 2 inches
- At least 3 inches
* At least 6 inches
- At least 12 inches
> Per the IRC (R401.3), the grade should fall at least 6 inches within the first 10 feet (about a 5% slope) so that surface water drains away from the foundation.

[h002] site
An inspector sees soil sloping toward the house along one wall, and the basement wall on that side shows white mineral deposits and a damp stain. What is the most appropriate report entry?
- State that the foundation is failing and recommend immediate replacement by a contractor
* Report the negative grading and moisture signs; recommend regrading away from the house
- Make no comment, because grading is a landscaping matter outside the scope of the inspection
- Recommend that the client install a dehumidifier in the basement and take no other action
> Grading that slopes toward the house is a common cause of basement moisture. The inspector should report it with the related evidence and recommend correcting the drainage. Nothing observed supports calling the foundation failed.

[h003] site
Per the IRC, impervious surfaces such as concrete walks within 10 feet of the building should slope away from it at a minimum of:
* 2 percent
- 0.5 percent
- 5 percent
- 10 percent
> Per the IRC (R401.3), impervious surfaces within 10 feet of the foundation may slope at not less than 2 percent away from the building. The 6-inch-in-10-feet (5%) rule applies to soil.

[h004] site
A downspout empties directly against the foundation at a corner where the basement shows water staining. What should the inspector recommend?
- Sealing the inside of the basement wall with waterproof paint to stop the leaks
- Removing the gutter system, because gutters concentrate water and cause foundation leaks
* Extending the downspout so that it discharges well away from the foundation
- Filling the area around the downspout with gravel and leaving the discharge where it is
> Roof water concentrated at the foundation is a frequent cause of basement leaks and settlement. Downspouts should discharge several feet from the foundation or into an approved drainage system. Interior sealers do not fix the source.

[h005] site
Per the IRC, what is the minimum clearance between wood siding and the ground, unless the wood is naturally durable or preservative-treated?
- 2 inches
- 4 inches
* 6 inches
- 8 inches
> Per the IRC (R317.1), wood siding, sheathing and wall framing on the exterior must be at least 6 inches above the ground (and at least 2 inches above concrete steps, patios and similar surfaces) unless decay-resistant wood is used.

[h006] site
Per the IRC, the weep screed at the bottom of an exterior stucco wall should be at least how far above earth and above paved areas?
- 1 inch above earth and 1 inch above paving
* 4 inches above earth and 2 inches above paving
- 6 inches above earth and 4 inches above paving
- 8 inches above earth and 6 inches above paving
> Per the IRC (R703.7.2.1), the foundation weep screed must be at least 4 inches above the earth or 2 inches above paved areas so that water behind the stucco can drain.

[h007] site
Per the IRC, weep holes in the outer wythe of brick veneer must be spaced no more than how far apart?
- 16 inches on center
- 24 inches on center
* 33 inches on center
- 48 inches on center
> Per the IRC (R703.8.6), weep holes are required immediately above the flashing at a maximum spacing of 33 inches on center, with a minimum diameter of 3/16 inch.

[h008] site
At the base of a brick veneer wall, the inspector cannot find any weep holes, and the bottom course is buried in mulch. What is the main concern?
* Water behind the veneer cannot drain and may damage sheathing and framing
- The brick will expand and crack because the wall cannot breathe without weeps
- Rodents and insects will enter the wall cavity through the buried openings
- The mortar joints will cure too slowly and remain soft for many years
> Brick veneer is a drainage wall: water that penetrates it must run down the air space and out through weeps above the flashing. Without weeps, or with them buried, the water is trapped against the wood structure.

[h009] site
What are the minimum diameter and the location of weep holes in brick veneer per the IRC?
- 1/8 inch, at mid-height of the wall
* 3/16 inch, immediately above the flashing
- 1/2 inch, just below the sill plate
- 3/4 inch, at the top course under the eave
> Per the IRC (R703.8.6), weep holes must be at least 3/16 inch in diameter and located immediately above the flashing, where water collected by the flashing exits.

[h010] site
An inspector finds barrier-type EIFS (synthetic stucco) that extends into the soil, with no kickout flashing where a roof edge meets the wall. Soft spots are felt below the roof-wall intersection. What is the most appropriate action?
- Report the cladding as acceptable, because EIFS is a waterproof barrier system
- Cut open the wall in the soft area to confirm rot before writing the report
* Report the conditions and refer to a qualified EIFS specialist for moisture testing
- Recommend recoating the EIFS with elastomeric paint to seal out the moisture
> Barrier EIFS cannot drain water that gets behind it, so hidden damage is common at roof-wall intersections and where the cladding meets grade. Destructive testing is beyond a home inspection; the correct step is referral to a specialist.

[h011] site
Vinyl siding shows waviness and buckled panels on a sunny wall. What installation error most likely caused this?
- Spacing the nails too far apart along the nailing hem
- Leaving small gaps at the ends of panels where they meet trim
* Driving the nails tight against the panels
- Installing the siding over house wrap instead of directly over sheathing
> Vinyl siding moves a lot with temperature. It must hang loosely on its nails with room to expand at the ends; nails driven tight pin the panels and cause buckling.

[h012] site
A deck ledger is fastened to the house only with nails and has no flashing above it. What should the inspector report?
- That the ledger is acceptable as long as the nails are hot-dip galvanized and closely spaced
- That the deck will perform well once a bead of caulk is added along the top of the ledger
* That the attachment and flashing are deficient and need correction by a qualified contractor
- That the ledger only needs to be painted or stained so that water cannot soak into it
> Ledger failure is a leading cause of deck collapse. Ledgers should be attached with lag screws or through-bolts (or another approved method) and flashed to keep water out of the house band joist. Nails alone are not an acceptable attachment.

[h013] site
Per the IRC, a deck ledger may NOT be supported on which of these?
- A preservative-treated band joist
- A solid concrete foundation wall
* Brick or stone veneer
- A properly sized dimensional-lumber rim joist
> Per the IRC (R507.9.2), deck ledgers must not be supported on stone or masonry veneer, because veneer is not designed to carry structural loads. A freestanding deck is the usual alternative.

[h014] site
Per the IRC, guards are required on a deck when the walking surface is more than how far above the grade below, measured within 36 inches horizontally of the edge?
- 18 inches
- 24 inches
* 30 inches
- 42 inches
> Per the IRC (R312.1.1), guards are required where the walking surface is more than 30 inches above the floor or grade below at any point within 36 inches horizontally of the edge.

[h015] site
Deck balusters are spaced so that a 5-inch ball passes between them. The deck is 6 feet above the yard. How should this be reported?
- As acceptable, because the IRC limit for openings is 6 inches
* As a safety hazard, because guard openings should not pass a 4-inch sphere per the IRC
- As acceptable, because baluster spacing rules apply only to interior stairs
- As a cosmetic issue that needs no recommendation
> Per the IRC (R312.1.3), openings in guards must not allow a 4-inch sphere to pass. Wider spacing lets small children slip through, so it is a safety item.

[h016] site
Several deck joist hangers have empty nail holes, and some are fastened with drywall screws. What is the best report comment?
- The hangers are adequate as long as each one has at least one fastener on each side
- Drywall screws are harder than nails, so using them is an upgrade over the original nailing
* The hangers are not fastened as the manufacturer requires and need correction
- The joists should be glued into the hangers with construction adhesive to stiffen them
> Joist hangers reach their rated capacity only with the specified fasteners in the specified holes. Drywall screws are brittle and are not rated for structural shear loads.

[h017] site
Per the IRC, fasteners and connectors used with preservative-treated lumber should be:
- Plain steel, because the preservative in the wood also protects the metal
- Aluminum, because it is lighter and does not rust like steel
* Hot-dip galvanized or stainless steel
- Electro-galvanized drywall screws, because their coating resists rust
> Per the IRC (R317.3), fasteners in preservative-treated wood must be hot-dip zinc-coated galvanized steel, stainless steel, silicon bronze or copper (with limited exceptions). Modern treatments corrode plain and lightly coated steel.

[h018] site
A 5-foot-tall landscape retaining wall near the house leans outward, bulges in the middle and has no visible drainage outlets. What is the most likely cause?
- The wall was built too thick for the amount of soil it holds
* Water and soil pressure behind a poorly drained wall
- Too much direct sunlight on the face of the wall during the summer
- The mortar was mixed with too much lime, making it too strong
> Water trapped behind a retaining wall adds a lot of pressure. Leaning and bulging point to inadequate drainage or design. The inspector should report it and recommend evaluation by a qualified professional, such as an engineer.

[h019] site
Per the IRC, retaining walls that are not laterally supported at the top must be designed for stability when they retain more than how much unbalanced fill?
- 24 inches
- 36 inches
* 48 inches
- 72 inches
> Per the IRC (R404.4), retaining walls not laterally supported at the top that retain more than 48 inches of unbalanced fill must be designed against overturning, sliding and excessive soil pressure.

[h020] site
Large tree limbs hang over the roof and rub the shingles. What is the most appropriate report comment?
- Leave it out, because trees are outside the scope of every standard of practice
- Recommend removing all trees within 50 feet of the house to protect the foundation
* Note the risk of roof damage and recommend trimming by a qualified tree service
- State that the tree is diseased and is likely to fall on the house in the next storm
> Standards of practice include vegetation that is likely to adversely affect the building. The inspector reports the effect and recommends trimming, without diagnosing tree health, which is outside the inspector's expertise.

[h021] site
Under typical home inspection standards of practice, which of these is the inspector NOT required to inspect?
- Driveways and walkways next to the house
- Exterior wall coverings
- Grading and drainage that may affect the building
* Swimming pools, spas and fences
> Typical standards of practice (such as ASHI and InterNACHI) cover wall coverings, flashing, trim, walkways, drives and the grading and vegetation that affect the building. Pools, spas, fences and other recreational facilities are excluded unless separately agreed.

[h022] site
Per the IRC, what is the minimum horizontal area of a window well that serves an emergency escape window?
- 5.7 square feet
* 9 square feet
- 12 square feet
- 16 square feet
> Per the IRC (R310.2.3.1 in the 2021 edition), a window well serving an emergency escape opening needs at least 9 square feet of horizontal area, with a minimum horizontal projection and width of 36 inches.

[h023] site
Per the IRC, an egress window well needs a permanently attached ladder or steps when its vertical depth is more than:
- 24 inches
- 36 inches
* 44 inches
- 60 inches
> Per the IRC, a window well deeper than 44 inches needs a permanently affixed ladder or steps that remain usable with the window fully open. The ladder must not intrude more than 6 inches into the required dimensions of the well.

[h024] site
A basement window well is full of leaves and standing water, and the window frame below it is stained. What is the most appropriate recommendation?
- Seal the window shut with caulk so that water in the well cannot leak inside
* Clean the well, restore its drainage, consider a cover, and check the window
- Fill the well with soil up to the bottom of the window to keep water out of it
- No action, because window wells are designed to hold water after a heavy rain
> A clogged window well collects water against the window. Restoring drainage, keeping debris out and checking the window address the cause. Sealing an egress window shut would create a safety problem.

[h025] site
Per the IRC, a door between an attached garage and the house may be which of these?
- A hollow-core wood door of any thickness
* A solid wood door at least 1-3/8 inches thick
- A sliding glass door
- Any door, as long as it has weatherstripping
> Per the IRC (R302.5.1), openings between the garage and the residence need solid wood or solid or honeycomb-core steel doors at least 1-3/8 inches thick, or 20-minute fire-rated doors. Since the 2018 edition the IRC also requires a self-closing or automatic-closing device.

[h026] site
The door from the garage into the house is a hollow-core interior door. How should the inspector report this?
- As acceptable, because any closed door slows the spread of a garage fire
- As a cosmetic issue to be handled whenever the door is next replaced
* As a fire-separation deficiency needing a compliant door
- As acceptable as long as a working smoke alarm is installed in the garage
> The garage-to-house door is part of the fire separation. A hollow-core door does not provide the required resistance, so the inspector should report it as a safety item.

[h027] site
Per the IRC, which opening is NOT permitted between an attached garage and the dwelling?
* A door from the garage directly into a bedroom
- A door from the garage into a mudroom
- A door from the garage into a kitchen
- A door from the garage into a hallway
> Per the IRC (R302.5.1), openings from a private garage directly into a room used for sleeping are not permitted.

[h028] site
Per the IRC, when there is a habitable room above an attached garage, the garage ceiling must have at least:
- 1/2-inch regular gypsum board
- 3/8-inch gypsum board
* 5/8-inch Type X gypsum board
- 1/2-inch plywood
> Per the IRC (Table R302.6), the garage side of walls needs at least 1/2-inch gypsum board, but where there is habitable space above the garage the ceiling needs at least 5/8-inch Type X gypsum board.

[h029] site
When the automatic garage door is tested, it does not reverse after it contacts an object on the floor. What is the most appropriate report entry?
* Report it as a safety hazard and recommend repair by a qualified technician
- Report it as normal wear for openers and doors that are more than 10 years old
- Recommend that the occupants keep children and pets out of the garage instead
- Disconnect the opener so that it cannot be used and leave it out of the report
> Automatic openers are designed to reverse when the door meets an obstruction. A failure to reverse can cause serious injury, so it is a safety finding that needs correction.

[h030] site
The photo-eye sensors for an automatic garage door are mounted about 4 feet above the floor. Why is this a concern?
- The sensors wear out faster when they are mounted far above the floor
- The door will refuse to close at all until the sensors are lowered
* A child or pet below the beam may not be detected
- The sensors may be confused by light from the garage ceiling fixtures
> Photo-eye safety sensors are meant to sense small objects near the floor; manufacturers and the UL 325 standard call for them to be mounted no more than about 6 inches above the floor.

[h031] site
In winter, a hose is still attached to a frost-free hose bibb. What risk should the inspector mention?
- The hose bibb will leak into the yard because the hose keeps its washer compressed
- The hose will make the bibb drip continuously and waste water all winter long
* Water held in the bibb can freeze and split the pipe inside the wall
- The attached hose will reduce water pressure to the other fixtures throughout the house
> A frost-free bibb works by draining the section of pipe in the cold wall. An attached hose keeps water in the valve, which can freeze and burst it inside the wall, where the leak may go unnoticed.

[h032] site
The inspector notices peeling paint and a soft-looking exterior window sill. What is an appropriate way to check it?
- Remove the sill and the trim below it to look at the wall framing behind them
* Gently probe the suspect area with an awl, without damaging sound finishes
- Leave it out of the report, because rot can only be confirmed by laboratory tests
- Tap the sill firmly with a hammer to see whether the wood breaks apart or holds
> Standards such as ASHI's call for probing where deterioration is suspected, but not where probing would damage finished surfaces that appear sound. A soft sill can be checked carefully and reported.

[h033] site
A concrete sidewalk slab has heaved and now has a 1-inch lip where it meets the next slab. How should this be reported?
- As cosmetic only
* As a trip hazard, recommending correction
- As a sign of foundation failure
- As a code violation that requires a permit
> A raised edge in a walking path is a trip hazard and should be reported as a safety item. A heaved walk alone does not show a problem with the house foundation.

[h034] site
Per the IRC, the landing at an exterior door must measure at least how far in the direction of travel?
- 24 inches
* 36 inches
- 42 inches
- 48 inches
> Per the IRC (R311.3), the floor or landing on each side of an exterior door must be at least as wide as the door and at least 36 inches in the direction of travel.

[h035] site
An exterior door does not swing over its outside landing. Per the IRC, how far below the top of the threshold may that landing be?
- No more than 1-1/2 inches
- No more than 4 inches
* No more than 7-3/4 inches
- No more than 12 inches
> Per the IRC (R311.3.1), the exterior landing may be no more than 7-3/4 inches below the top of the threshold, provided the door does not swing over it. That is the same as the maximum riser height.

[h036] site
Fiber-cement siding was installed down to the soil and tight against a roof surface. What is the best basis for reporting this?
- No comment is needed, because fiber cement does not absorb water or decay
* It does not meet the manufacturer's clearance instructions
- Fiber cement must always be at least 18 inches above grade under every code
- Fiber cement may never be installed on any wall that is next to a roof surface
> Fiber-cement products can be damaged by constant moisture, and manufacturers specify clearances to grade, hard surfaces and roofs. Reporting a departure from the manufacturer's instructions is defensible and specific.

[h037] site
Mulch has been piled against the house so that it covers the bottom of the wood siding. What two problems does this create?
- Higher water pressure at the hose bibbs and lower heating bills in winter
* Wood decay and a hidden termite path
- Faster paint drying, along with color fading of the lower siding courses
- Better drainage of roof water and extra insulation for the foundation wall
> Soil or mulch against wood cladding keeps it wet and hides termite entry points. Clearance between cladding and grade should be restored.

[h038] site
What is the main purpose of head flashing (drip cap) above a window in a siding wall?
- To support the weight of the siding courses that run above the window opening
* To shed water running down the wall away from the window head
- To keep insects and nesting birds from getting into the top of the window frame
- To cover the gap between the window and the siding for a neater appearance
> Head flashing intercepts water running down the wall and sheds it over the window trim. If it is missing, water can get behind the trim and into the framing.

[h039] site
Cement shingle siding on a 1950s house may contain asbestos. What is the most appropriate way to report it?
- State in the report that the siding contains asbestos and must be removed
- Break off a small piece of siding and send it to a laboratory for analysis
* Describe the siding and note that only lab testing can confirm asbestos
- Leave it out of the report, because environmental hazards may not be mentioned
> Standards of practice do not require the inspector to identify environmental hazards. Taking samples could release fibers. Describing the material and advising that testing is needed for confirmation is accurate and within scope.

[h040] site
Stucco below several windows shows cracks with brown staining underneath. What is the most appropriate recommendation?
- Painting over the stains with a stain-blocking primer and matching paint
- Filling the cracks with caulk and taking no further action on the stains
* Evaluation by a qualified stucco or moisture specialist
- Removing all of the stucco from the house and replacing it with vinyl siding
> Stains below window corners often indicate leaks at the window flashing that can cause hidden damage. Specialists can do invasive moisture testing, which is beyond a visual inspection.

[h041] site
A wooden privacy fence is attached directly to the house siding. What is the main concern?
- The fence will make the house look smaller and lower its curb appeal
- The fence will raise the property taxes because it counts as an addition
* It can give termites a hidden path and keep the siding wet
- The fence will block the electric meter and the utility will refuse to read it
> Wood that bridges from the ground to the house can carry moisture and give wood-destroying insects a path into the structure. Separating the fence from the house is a common recommendation.

[h042] site
A lawn sprinkler head sprays the lower part of a wood-sided wall every morning. What is the best recommendation?
- Install a gutter at the bottom of the wall
* Adjust the sprinkler so that it does not wet the house
- Paint the siding every year
- Remove all landscaping
> Repeated wetting of siding and the foundation encourages rot, staining and moisture entry. Redirecting the spray removes the source.

[h043] site
An inspector is asked to describe the exterior. Which of these is an exterior item that standards of practice typically require the inspector to inspect?
- The condition of a detached shed
- Window screens and storm windows
* Eaves, soffits and fascia
- Outdoor cooking appliances
> Eaves, soffits and fascia are part of the exterior that standards of practice cover. Outbuildings, screens, storm windows and outdoor cooking equipment are commonly excluded.

[h044] site
Shrubs are growing against the siding on the north side of the house, and the siding there is green with algae. What is the best recommendation?
* Trim the vegetation back so that the wall can dry and be inspected
- Remove the siding on that wall and replace it with brick veneer instead
- Plant more shrubs along the wall to shade it from the afternoon sun
- No action, because algae growth shows that the siding is healthy and sound
> Vegetation against the house keeps the wall damp and hides defects and pest activity. Keeping plants trimmed back is standard advice.

[h045] site
A cracked concrete driveway has hairline cracks but no displacement or trip edges. What is the most accurate description?
- A structural defect that should be evaluated by a structural engineer
* Typical shrinkage cracking, usually of little concern
- Evidence that a sinkhole is forming under the driveway and the garage
- A sign that the house foundation is failing and moving toward the street
> Concrete flatwork almost always develops shrinkage cracks. Without displacement, trip edges or drainage problems, hairline cracks are usually minor.

[h046] site
Which observation best indicates a surface drainage problem next to the foundation?
* Soil erosion channels and water marks running toward the house
- A well-sloped lawn
- Downspout extensions that reach 6 feet from the house
- A dry crawl space with a vapor retarder
> Erosion channels, water marks and puddling at the foundation show that water flows toward the house rather than away from it.

[h047] site
A brick wall shows white, powdery deposits near the bottom. What do these deposits usually indicate?
- Mold growing on the face of the bricks because of shade
- Termites tunneling through the mortar joints of the wall
* Salts left behind by water moving through the masonry
- Fire damage from a past fire that scorched the wall surface
> White, powdery deposits on masonry are efflorescence: salts that water carried through the masonry and left on the surface as it evaporated. The deposits show moisture movement. The source should be found.

[h048] site
Deck stairs have no handrail and five risers. What does the IRC require?
- No handrail, because stairs serving decks are exempt from handrail rules
* A handrail on at least one side
- A handrail on both sides of every exterior stair regardless of the number of risers
- A handrail only if the stairs are more than 6 feet wide or more than 10 risers tall
> Per the IRC (R311.7.8), handrails are required on at least one side of each flight of stairs with four or more risers. Exterior deck stairs are included.

[h049] site
A deck's wood posts sit directly on the soil, and their bases are soft and decayed. What is the best recommendation?
- Paint or stain the posts to seal the decayed wood against further moisture
* Repair by a qualified contractor, with posts set on proper footings
- Add more posts beside the decayed ones, set directly on the soil without footings
- No action, because wood posts are expected to rot slowly and are still adequate
> Posts in direct contact with soil decay, and decayed posts can fail. Posts should bear on footings and be protected from ground moisture; a contractor should repair them.

[h050] site
A home inspector is asked by the buyer whether the lot is in a flood zone. What is the most appropriate response?
- Give an opinion based on the slope of the street and the yard around the house
- Guarantee in writing that the house will not flood during the client's ownership
* Explain it is outside the inspection and suggest flood maps or the insurer
- Say that every lot within a mile of a creek or river is in a flood zone
> Determining flood zone status, geological conditions and soil stability is outside a standard home inspection. Referring the client to the proper source avoids giving an unsupported opinion.

# ---------------- Roofing (h051-h100) ----------------

[h051] roof
Per the IRC, what is the minimum roof slope for asphalt shingles?
- 1 unit vertical in 12 horizontal (1:12)
* 2 units vertical in 12 horizontal (2:12)
- 3 units vertical in 12 horizontal (3:12)
- 4 units vertical in 12 horizontal (4:12)
> Per the IRC (R905.2.2), asphalt shingles may be used only on roof slopes of 2:12 or greater. Slopes from 2:12 up to less than 4:12 need double underlayment.

[h052] roof
Per the IRC, asphalt shingles on a slope of 3:12 require:
- No underlayment
- A single layer of underlayment, the same as steeper roofs
* Two layers of underlayment (double underlayment)
- A fully adhered membrane over the whole roof, with no shingles
> Per the IRC, shingles on slopes from 2:12 to less than 4:12 need two layers of underlayment, because water drains more slowly and wind can drive it under low-slope shingles.

[h053] roof
Per the IRC, where an ice barrier is required, it must extend from the lowest edges of the roof to a point at least how far inside the exterior wall line?
- 6 inches
- 12 inches
* 24 inches
- 36 inches
> Per the IRC (R905.1.2), in areas with a history of ice damming the ice barrier must run from the eave to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line of the building.

[h054] roof
Per the IRC, a new layer of asphalt shingles may NOT be installed over the existing roof (re-covered) when:
- The existing roof has one layer of shingles
* The existing roof already has two or more layers
- The existing shingles are less than 10 years old
- The roof slope is greater than 6:12
> Per the IRC (R908.3.1.1), re-covering is not permitted where the existing roof has two or more applications of any type of roof covering, so a roof normally has at most two layers.

[h055] roof
Per the IRC, which existing roof covering may NOT be covered over with new shingles, even if it is the only layer?
- A single layer of asphalt shingles in good condition
* Wood shakes, slate or clay tile
- A single layer of fiberglass three-tab shingles
- Architectural asphalt shingles that lie flat
> Per the IRC (R908.3.1.1), re-covering is not permitted over wood shake, slate, clay, cement or asbestos-cement tile, as well as over roofs that are water-soaked or badly deteriorated.

[h056] roof
Per the IRC, what is the minimum slope for wood shingles?
- 1:12
- 2:12
* 3:12
- 4:12
> Per the IRC (R905.7.1 for wood shingles and R905.8.1 for wood shakes), wood shingles and shakes need a slope of at least 3:12.

[h057] roof
Per the IRC, what is the minimum slope for clay or concrete roof tile?
- 1:12
- 2:12
* 2-1/2:12
- 5:12
> Per the IRC (R905.3.2), clay and concrete tile need a slope of at least 2-1/2:12. Slopes from 2-1/2:12 to less than 4:12 need double underlayment.

[h058] roof
Per the IRC, what is the minimum slope for a built-up (tar and gravel) roof, other than coal-tar?
- Flat (0:12)
- 1/8 inch per foot
* 1/4 inch per foot (1/4:12)
- 2 inches per foot (2:12)
> Per the IRC (R905.9.1), built-up roofs need a design slope of at least 1/4 unit in 12 (2%) for drainage. Coal-tar built-up roofs may be 1/8:12.

[h059] roof
Per the IRC, mineral-surfaced roll roofing must not be applied on slopes below:
* 1:12
- 2:12
- 3:12
- 4:12
> Per the IRC (R905.5.2), mineral-surfaced roll roofing must not be applied on slopes below 1 unit vertical in 12 units horizontal.

[h060] roof
Per the IRC, standing-seam metal roof systems may be installed down to a minimum slope of:
- 3:12
- 2:12
- 1:12
* 1/4:12
> Per the IRC (R905.10.2), standing-seam metal roofs may go down to 1/4:12. Lapped, non-soldered seam metal roofs need 3:12, or 1/2:12 with applied lap sealant.

[h061] roof
Per the IRC, where must drip edge be installed on an asphalt shingle roof?
- Only along the eaves, where water drips off the roof edge into the gutters
- Only along the rake (gable) edges, where wind-driven rain is the main concern
* Along both the eaves and the rakes
- Only where gutters are installed, because the gutter replaces the drip edge elsewhere
> Per the IRC (R905.2.8.5), drip edge is required at eaves and rake edges of shingle roofs. Adjacent pieces overlap at least 2 inches, and the drip edge extends at least 1/4 inch below the roof sheathing.

[h062] roof
Siding below the bottom end of a roof-to-wall intersection is stained and soft. There is no diverter flashing where the roof edge meets the wall. What flashing is missing?
- Valley flashing
- Counterflashing at the chimney
* Kickout (diverter) flashing
- Drip edge at the rake
> Per the IRC (R903.2.1), a kickout (diverter) flashing is required where the eave of a roof meets a sidewall. Without it, water running down the step flashing flows into the wall instead of into the gutter.

[h063] roof
Per the IRC, step flashing where a shingle roof meets a sidewall must be at least:
- 2 inches high and 2 inches wide
* 4 inches high and 4 inches wide
- 6 inches high and 8 inches wide
- 10 inches high and 10 inches wide
> Per the IRC (R905.2.8.3), base flashing against a vertical sidewall, whether continuous or step flashing, must be at least 4 inches high and 4 inches wide.

[h064] roof
Under the IRC and typical manufacturer instructions, how many fasteners are normally required for each asphalt strip shingle on a typical-slope roof?
- 2
- 3
* 4
- 8
> Per the IRC (R905.2.6), asphalt strip shingles need at least four fasteners each, or more where the manufacturer requires (for example, in high-wind areas or on very steep slopes).

[h065] roof
Many shingles on a 22-year-old roof have bare patches where granules are gone, and lots of granules are collecting at the downspouts. What is the most likely explanation?
- Hail damage from a storm within the past week or two
* Normal aging, suggesting the roof is near the end of its life
- Poor attic insulation that lets heat melt the granule adhesive
- Installation over only a single layer of underlayment instead of two
> Widespread, even granule loss on an older roof is a sign of aging. The inspector should report it and recommend that a roofer evaluate the roof, rather than predicting an exact remaining life.

[h066] roof
Shingles on several slopes show randomly spaced dark spots where granules are missing, and the mat underneath feels soft when pressed. Metal roof vents have small dents. What is the most likely cause?
* Hail impact
- Foot traffic from installers
- Manufacturing blisters
- Algae growth
> Random bruises with soft spots and dents in soft metals are typical signs of hail. The inspector should report possible storm damage and recommend evaluation by a roofer (the client may also want to contact the insurer).

[h067] roof
After a windstorm, an inspector sees shingle tabs that are creased across the top, and a few tabs are missing. What is the most likely cause?
- Hail
- Ice damming
* Wind lifting the tabs
- Overheating from poor ventilation
> Wind lifts and folds tabs, creasing them and breaking the seal, and can tear tabs off. Creased or missing tabs should be reported for repair.

[h068] roof
Dark streaks run down the north slope of an asphalt shingle roof, but the shingles lie flat and are intact. What is the most likely cause, and how serious is it?
- Rot in the roof deck; serious
* Algae growth; mainly cosmetic
- Hail damage; serious
- Overheated shingles from poor attic ventilation; serious
> Dark streaking on shady slopes is usually algae (often Gloeocapsa magma). It is mostly a cosmetic issue, unlike moss, which holds moisture and can lift shingles.

[h069] roof
Thick moss is growing on a shaded asphalt shingle roof. Why should this be reported?
- Moss makes the roof surface more fire-resistant and should be left alone
* Moss holds moisture and can lift shingles, shortening roof life
- Moss growth is a reliable sign that the roof was damaged by hail recently
- Moss always means that the roof deck below it has rotted through completely
> Moss retains water and its growth can lift shingle edges, allowing water entry and speeding wear. Removal by a roofer is commonly recommended.

[h070] roof
The neoprene boot around a plumbing vent on the roof is cracked, and there is a water stain on the ceiling below it. What is the most likely source?
- Condensation forming on the underside of the roof sheathing in the attic
- A leaking water supply pipe running inside the wall next to the ceiling
* Water entering through the cracked vent boot
- A clogged gutter overflowing onto the soffit and running back into the ceiling
> Rubber vent boots break down in sunlight over time. A cracked boot directly above a ceiling stain is the most likely leak source and should be replaced.

[h071] roof
Nail heads are exposed through several shingles, and some have pushed up the shingle above them. What should the inspector recommend?
- No action, because exposed nail heads are normal on most asphalt roofs
* Repair by a roofer, because the nails can let water in
- Painting the nail heads with roof paint so that they blend in with the shingles
- Replacing the gutters, because nail pops are caused by overflowing gutters
> Nail pops and exposed fasteners can let water past the shingles. They are a common, repairable defect that should be reported.

[h072] roof
Shingle nails were placed well above the manufacturer's nailing line. What problem can this cause?
* Reduced wind resistance and holding power
- Too much granule loss from the shingle surface
- Algae growth along the shingle tabs on shaded slopes
- Ice damming at the eaves during the following winter
> Manufacturers specify a nailing zone so that each nail goes through the overlap and holds two layers. High nailing reduces holding power and can lead to blow-offs.

[h073] roof
A chimney has only roofing tar smeared around its base, with no step flashing or counterflashing, and the ceiling next to the chimney is stained. What should the inspector report?
- That the tar is a permanent, acceptable flashing method if it is renewed every year
* That proper flashing is missing and should be corrected by a roofer or mason
- That the chimney must be removed down to below the roof line and rebuilt
- That the ceiling stain is from condensation and is not related to the chimney at all
> Roof cement alone is a temporary patch that cracks and fails. Chimneys need step (base) flashing plus counterflashing set into the masonry. The stain supports the conclusion that water is getting in.

[h074] roof
Per the IRC, a cricket (saddle) is required on the upslope side of a chimney when the chimney's dimension parallel to the ridge is more than:
- 12 inches
- 24 inches
* 30 inches
- 48 inches
> Per the IRC (R1003.20 and R903.2.2), a cricket is required where the chimney dimension parallel to the ridge is greater than 30 inches, unless the chimney is at the ridge, to divert water and debris around it.

[h075] roof
In the roofing industry, standing water on a low-slope roof is usually called "ponding" when it remains how long after rain?
- More than 2 hours
- More than 12 hours
* More than 48 hours
- More than 2 weeks
> Industry practice (such as NRCA's) commonly calls water that remains more than 48 hours after rain, under conditions that allow drying, ponding. It speeds deterioration and points to poor drainage or sagging.

[h076] roof
The surface of an older built-up roof shows a pattern of deep cracks that looks like reptile skin. What is this called, and what causes it?
- Blistering, caused by moisture trapped between the plies of the roof
* Alligatoring, from the asphalt aging in the sun
- Fishmouthing, caused by poorly installed felt laps at the seams of the roof
- Ridging, caused by movement of the roof deck under the membrane over time
> Alligatoring is surface cracking of asphalt as it ages and becomes brittle from UV exposure. It shows the surfacing is worn out and needs maintenance or replacement.

[h077] roof
On a modified bitumen roof, the inspector sees raised, soft bubbles in the membrane. What are these, and what is a common cause?
- Fishmouths, caused by loose edge flashing lifting along the perimeter of the roof
* Blisters, from trapped moisture or air that expands
- Splits, caused by heavy foot traffic from service workers crossing the roof
- Alligatoring, caused by sun damage that has dried out the membrane surface
> Blisters are pockets where the membrane has separated, usually because moisture or air was trapped and expands with heat. They should be reported because they can rupture.

[h078] roof
An EPDM (rubber) roof has seams that are peeling apart at the laps. What is the risk?
- None, because EPDM membranes seal themselves at the laps over time
* Water can enter at the open seams and leak inside
- The roof will absorb more heat and become too hot in the summer months
- The roof will become too heavy for the framing because of the loose sheets
> EPDM is laid in sheets joined at seams with adhesive or tape. Open seams are a common leak point and should be repaired by a qualified roofer.

[h079] roof
The roof is slate. Several slates are cracked and one has slipped out of place. How should the inspector inspect this roof?
* From the ground, a ladder at the eaves, or a drone
- By walking on the roof, as on any asphalt shingle roof of a similar slope
- By removing a few slates to check the condition of the underlayment beneath
- It should not be inspected at all, and the report should not mention the roof
> Slate is brittle and easily broken underfoot. Inspectors usually view it from the ground, the eaves or a drone, and report the method used.

[h080] roof
Under typical standards of practice, which statement about inspecting a roof is correct?
- The inspector must walk on every roof, regardless of slope or weather
- The inspector must certify in writing that the roof will not leak
* The inspector need not walk the roof but must describe the method used
- The inspector must estimate the roof's remaining life to the nearest year
> Standards such as ASHI and InterNACHI let the inspector choose a safe method, such as walking, using binoculars or a drone, and require reporting the method used. They do not require walking the roof, certifying it or predicting remaining life.

[h081] roof
It is raining, and the roof is a steep 12:12 slope with wet, slippery shingles. What is the most appropriate action?
- Walk the roof carefully anyway, because the client paid for a full roof inspection
- Skip the roof entirely and leave any mention of it out of the written report
* Inspect from the ground, a ladder or a drone, and report the method and limits
- Reschedule the whole inspection for the summer, when the roof will be dry and warm
> Inspectors are not required to do anything unsafe. They should use another method and clearly state the method and its limits in the report.

[h082] roof
Shingles on a 10-year-old roof are curling up at the edges (cupping) and down at the corners (clawing) across the whole roof. Which condition can speed this up?
- A roof slope of 8:12, which is steeper than average
* Poor attic ventilation that traps heat
- Gutters that are too large for the size of the roof
- Algae growing on the north-facing slopes of the roof
> Curling is a sign of aging, and excess heat and moisture from an under-ventilated attic can speed it up. The inspector should report the curling and look for attic ventilation problems.

[h083] roof
Viewed from the attic, the roof sheathing between rafters dips noticeably, and the roof felt spongy when walked on. What is the most likely condition?
- Normal deflection that is found in all roof sheathing between rafters
* Deteriorated or undersized roof sheathing
- Framing that is too stiff, so the shingles cannot flex with the wind
- An extra layer of shingles that makes the roof stronger and safer
> Sagging sheathing and a spongy feel suggest delaminated, rotted or overspanned decking. The inspector should report it and recommend evaluation by a roofer or contractor.

[h084] roof
Per the IRC, what is the basic required net free ventilating area for a vented attic?
- 1/50 of the vented space
- 1/100 of the vented space
* 1/150 of the vented space
- 1/500 of the vented space
> Per the IRC (R806.2), the minimum net free ventilating area is 1/150 of the area of the vented space, which may be reduced to 1/300 when the conditions in the exception are met.

[h085] roof
Per the IRC, attic ventilation may be reduced from 1/150 to 1/300 when, among other options, at least 40% and no more than 50% of the required vent area is in the upper part of the attic. How far below the ridge may those upper vents be?
- No more than 1 foot
* No more than 3 feet
- No more than 6 feet
- Anywhere in the attic
> Per the IRC (R806.2), the upper vents must be no more than 3 feet below the ridge or highest point of the space, measured vertically, with the rest of the required ventilation at the eaves or cornice.

[h086] roof
An attic has 1,500 square feet of vented space. Per the IRC basic 1/150 ratio, how much net free ventilating area is required?
- 5 square feet
* 10 square feet
- 15 square feet
- 150 square feet
> 1,500 divided by 150 equals 10 square feet of net free area. If the 1/300 exception applies, the requirement would be 5 square feet.

[h087] roof
Per the IRC, openings in attic ventilation covers (screens) must be at least 1/16 inch and no more than:
- 1/8 inch
* 1/4 inch
- 1/2 inch
- 1 inch
> Per the IRC (R806.1), ventilation openings must have corrosion-resistant mesh or similar covers with openings between 1/16 inch and 1/4 inch, which keeps out animals but still lets air flow.

[h088] roof
Per the IRC, where insulation could block eave vents, a baffle must keep an air space of at least how much between the insulation and the roof sheathing at the vent?
* 1 inch
- 1/4 inch
- 3 inches
- 6 inches
> Per the IRC (R806.3), where eave or cornice vents are installed, insulation must not block free airflow, and a minimum 1-inch space must be kept between the insulation and the roof sheathing at the vent.

[h089] roof
In winter, thick ice builds up along the eaves, and water stains show up on the ceiling near exterior walls. What is the usual root cause?
- Gutters that are too large for the roof, so they fill with snow
* Attic heat melting snow that refreezes at the cold eaves
- Shingles installed with too many nails, which conduct cold into the roof
- A roof slope that is too steep, so snow slides down and piles up at the eaves
> Ice dams form when warm attic surfaces melt snow that refreezes at the cold overhang. Air sealing, insulation and ventilation are the long-term fixes; an ice barrier limits leaks.

[h090] roof
In the attic, the inspector finds frost and dark staining on the sheathing right above a bathroom exhaust fan that has no duct. What is the most appropriate recommendation?
- Add more insulation on top of the fan housing to stop the condensation
* Have the fan ducted to the exterior
- Turn the fan off permanently and rely on opening the bathroom door instead
- Install a second fan in the attic to blow the moist air out the gable vent
> Per the IRC (M1501.1), exhaust air must go to the outdoors, not into the attic. Warm, moist air from the bathroom condenses on cold sheathing and causes mold and decay.

[h091] roof
How can an inspector usually estimate how many layers of shingles are on a roof without removing any?
- By weighing a single shingle removed from the field of the roof
- By measuring the attic temperature on a sunny afternoon
* By looking at the roof edges and penetrations
- By counting the number of nails used in one shingle
> Layers can often be counted where the roof edges are visible, at drip edges, rakes or around vents. This is a non-destructive way to estimate the number of roof coverings.

[h092] roof
The first course of shingles at the eave has no starter strip, so water can run through the tab cutouts onto the sheathing. What is the best report entry?
- Acceptable, because starter strips are an optional upgrade on shingle roofs
* Improper installation; recommend repair by a qualified roofer
- The gutters should be removed so that water can drain freely off the eave
- More attic insulation should be installed to keep the eave from leaking
> Manufacturers require a starter course so that the gaps between the first course's tabs are covered and the first course is sealed down. Without it, water and wind can get under the first course.

[h093] roof
A gutter is full of debris and overflowing, and the fascia behind it is rotted. What should the inspector recommend?
* Cleaning, fascia repair and routine gutter maintenance
- Adding a second downspout at the other end while leaving the debris in place
- Painting the gutters and fascia with a water-resistant exterior coating
- Filling the low spots in the gutter with sealant so that water flows faster
> Clogged gutters overflow behind the gutter, rot the fascia and send water to the foundation. Cleaning, repairing the damage and routine maintenance solve the problem.

[h094] roof
On a concrete tile roof, what usually provides the main waterproofing?
- The tiles alone
* The underlayment beneath the tiles
- The paint on the tiles
- The battens that hold the tiles
> Tiles shed most water, but water gets past the joints, so the underlayment is the main waterproofing layer. Old underlayment often fails before the tiles do.

[h095] roof
What is a closed-cut valley?
- A valley lined with exposed metal, where no shingles cross the valley center
* One slope's shingles run through the valley; the other slope's are cut along a line
- A valley where the shingles from both slopes are interlaced (woven) together
- A valley that is sealed with roof tar and is left without any shingles at all
> In a closed-cut valley, one slope's shingles run through the valley and the other slope's shingles are trimmed along a line. Open valleys show metal; woven valleys interlace both slopes.

[h096] roof
A satellite dish has been bolted directly through the shingles in the middle of a roof slope, with no flashing. What is the concern?
- The dish may lower the roof temperature
* The fastener holes can let water into the roof
- Satellite dishes attract lightning
- The dish voids the house's electrical service
> Penetrations through roofing need proper flashing or sealing. Bolts through shingles are a common leak source; the dish itself is outside the inspection, but its effect on the roof is not.

[h097] roof
A skylight has water stains on the drywall around its interior shaft. What should the inspector do?
* Report the stains and recommend evaluation of the skylight by a roofer
- State that all skylights eventually leak and that this one must be removed
- Say nothing, because skylights are excluded from every standard of practice
- Recommend painting over the stain with stain-blocking primer and moving on
> Skylights are a frequent leak point at the curb flashing or the glazing seals. The inspector should report the evidence; a moisture meter can show whether the area is wet at the time of inspection.

[h098] roof
Why might new asphalt shingles installed in cold weather not seal down properly?
- The roofing nails contract and rust in cold weather, loosening the shingles
* The sealant strips need heat to bond
- The shingles shrink permanently in the cold, so the courses no longer overlap
- The granules fall off in cold weather, which prevents the shingles from sealing
> Self-sealing strips are activated by heat. In cold weather, shingles may stay unsealed and vulnerable to wind until temperatures rise, unless hand-sealed per the manufacturer.

[h099] roof
Under typical standards of practice, which of these is the inspector NOT required to do when inspecting a roof?
- Inspect flashings around roof penetrations
- Inspect the roof covering
- Report the method used to inspect the roof
* Remove snow or ice to see the roof surface
> Typical standards of practice require inspecting the roof covering, flashings, gutters and penetrations and describing the method used. They do not require removing snow, ice or debris, which is a limitation to report.

[h100] roof
A low-slope roof has a single interior drain, and the area around it holds water. What additional feature is commonly needed so that the roof can drain if that drain clogs?
- A larger gutter along the edge of the roof
* A secondary (overflow) drain or scupper
- A thicker roof membrane over the whole roof
- More insulation below the roof deck
> Roofs that drain to interior drains or are surrounded by parapets need secondary drains or scuppers so that a clogged primary drain does not cause dangerous ponding or leaks.

[h101] structure
Per the IRC, the bottom of an exterior footing must be at least how far below the surface of undisturbed ground, regardless of frost depth?
- 6 inches
* 12 inches
- 24 inches
- 36 inches
> Per the IRC (R403.1.4), exterior footings must be at least 12 inches below undisturbed ground and also below the frost line set by the local jurisdiction, unless frost-protected by another approved method.

[h102] structure
Per the IRC, what are the minimum diameter and maximum spacing of anchor bolts that fasten a wood sill plate to a foundation?
- 3/8 inch, 8 feet on center
* 1/2 inch, 6 feet on center
- 5/8 inch, 10 feet on center
- 1/4 inch, 4 feet on center
> Per the IRC (R403.1.6), anchor bolts must be at least 1/2 inch in diameter, spaced no more than 6 feet apart, embedded at least 7 inches, with a bolt within 12 inches of each end of each plate section.

[h103] structure
Per the IRC, an anchor bolt must be located within how far of each end of each sill plate section?
- 4 inches
* 12 inches
- 24 inches
- 36 inches
> Per the IRC (R403.1.6), there must be at least two bolts per plate section, with one bolt within 12 inches of each end of each section.

[h104] structure
A concrete block basement wall has a long horizontal crack about halfway up, and the wall bows inward along the crack. What is the most likely cause, and what should the inspector recommend?
- Normal concrete shrinkage; no action is needed beyond sealing the crack
* Lateral soil and water pressure; refer to a structural engineer
- A roof leak running down inside the wall; repair the roof flashing first
- Frost forming in the attic and loading the walls; add attic insulation
> A horizontal crack with inward bowing in a block wall is a classic sign of lateral pressure from soil and water. It can get worse and is a significant structural concern that needs an engineer.

[h105] structure
A brick or concrete block wall shows a crack that zigzags along the mortar joints in a stair-step pattern. What does this usually indicate?
* Differential settlement or movement of the foundation
- Normal shrinkage of the mortar as it cured years ago
- Termite tunnels running through the mortar joints
- Too much insulation installed against the inside face
> Stair-step cracking through mortar joints usually shows that one part of the wall moved relative to another, often from settlement. Width, displacement and signs of ongoing movement determine how serious it is.

[h106] structure
A poured concrete basement wall has a single narrow vertical crack with no displacement and no water entry. What is the most likely cause?
- Lateral soil pressure pushing the wall inward
* Shrinkage as the concrete cured
- An overloaded roof bearing down on the wall
- Frost heave lifting the footing in winter
> Narrow vertical cracks in poured walls are typically from shrinkage and are usually not structurally significant. They can still let water in, and wide, displaced or growing cracks need further evaluation.

[h107] structure
Interior drywall shows diagonal cracks running up from the corners of several doors and windows, and some doors stick. What is the most likely explanation?
- Paint that was applied too thickly over the joints
* Structural movement, such as settlement
- High-quality drywall finishing that is too rigid
- A plumbing leak in the attic running down the walls
> Openings are weak points where movement shows up as diagonal cracks and doors that bind. The inspector should report the pattern and look for its cause in the foundation and framing.

[h108] structure
Bricks on a chimney and on the lower part of a wall have faces that are flaking off (spalling). What usually causes this?
- Termites tunneling through the brick faces
* Absorbed water freezing and thawing
- Too much mortar packed into the joints
- Lightning strikes on the chimney over time
> Spalling happens when absorbed water freezes and expands, breaking off the brick face. It indicates excess moisture, such as from missing caps, poor drainage or hard, impermeable mortar.

[h109] structure
Per the IRC, notches in the top or bottom of solid sawn floor joists must not exceed what fraction of the joist depth, and must not be in which part of the span?
- 1/3 of the depth; the outer third
- 1/4 of the depth; the end sixth
* 1/6 of the depth; the middle third
- 1/2 of the depth; the middle sixth
> Per the IRC (R502.8.1), notches in solid lumber joists must not exceed 1/6 of the depth, must not be longer than 1/3 of the depth, and must not be in the middle third of the span. Notches at the ends must not exceed 1/4 of the depth.

[h110] structure
Per the IRC, bored holes in solid sawn floor joists must not be within how far of the top or bottom edge, and must not exceed what diameter?
- 1/2 inch of the edge; 1/2 of the joist depth
- 1 inch of the edge; 1/4 of the joist depth
* 2 inches of the edge; 1/3 of the joist depth
- 3 inches of the edge; 2/3 of the joist depth
> Per the IRC (R502.8.1), holes in solid joists must be no closer than 2 inches to the top or bottom and no larger than 1/3 of the joist depth.

[h111] structure
In a crawl space, the inspector sees that a floor joist has been notched several inches deep on the bottom edge near mid-span to fit a drain pipe. How should this be reported?
- As acceptable, because the drain pipe needed the space to slope properly
* As a structural defect needing repair by a contractor or engineer
- As a cosmetic issue, because the notch is hidden in the crawl space
- As a plumbing defect only, to be corrected by moving the drain pipe later
> A deep notch on the bottom (tension) edge in the middle third of the span greatly weakens a joist and is not allowed by the IRC. It should be reported for repair, such as sistering or a header.

[h112] structure
Per the IRC, in an exterior or bearing wall, a stud may be bored with a hole no larger than what percentage of its width, unless it is doubled?
- 25 percent
* 40 percent
- 60 percent
- 75 percent
> Per the IRC (R602.6), bored holes in bearing-wall studs may be up to 40% of the stud width, or up to 60% if the stud is doubled and no more than two successive studs are bored. Notches are limited to 25%.

[h113] structure
Per the IRC, in a bearing wall, a stud may be notched no more than what percentage of its width?
* 25 percent
- 40 percent
- 50 percent
- 60 percent
> Per the IRC (R602.6), studs in exterior or bearing walls may be notched up to 25% of their width. Nonbearing partition studs may be notched up to 40%.

[h114] structure
Per the IRC, when a pipe cuts through more than 50% of the width of a top plate, what is required?
- Nothing, as long as the cut is in a wall that carries a roof load
- Filling the hole around the pipe with fire-rated spray foam only
* A metal strap across the cut, fastened on each side
- Replacing the whole top plate along the length of the wall
> Per the IRC (R602.6.1), where piping or ductwork cuts more than 50% of a top plate's width in an exterior or bearing wall, a galvanized metal strap must be fastened across and to the plate on each side of the opening.

[h115] structure
In the attic, the inspector finds that a web member of a manufactured roof truss has been cut to make room for an air handler. What is the most appropriate action?
- Report it as acceptable as long as the cut is clean and square
- Nail a short board beside the cut, then continue the inspection without comment
* Report it as a defect and refer it to an engineer or the truss manufacturer
- Recommend adding insulation around the cut to protect the truss from moisture
> Trusses are engineered systems; cutting or altering any member can compromise the whole truss. Repairs require a design from an engineer or the truss manufacturer.

[h116] structure
The bottom flange of an I-joist has been notched to fit a duct. What is the concern?
* The flange must not be cut, so the joist may be seriously weakened
- None, because I-joists are much stronger than solid lumber joists
- Only a cosmetic concern, because the flange is hidden by the ceiling
- The duct will be too small for the airflow needed by the room above
> Manufacturers prohibit cutting or notching I-joist flanges. Only holes in the web are allowed, at the sizes and locations the manufacturer specifies. A cut flange is a structural defect.

[h117] structure
What is the main structural purpose of rafter ties (or ceiling joists tied to the rafters) in a conventionally framed gable roof?
- To support attic storage
* To resist the rafters' outward thrust on the walls
- To provide nailing for the ceiling drywall only
- To hold down the roof against wind uplift
> Rafter ties connect the rafter ends to keep the walls from spreading apart under the roof load. Collar ties in the upper third of the attic mainly resist wind uplift and ridge separation.

[h118] structure
The ridge of a stick-framed roof sags, and the top of the exterior walls leans outward. There are no ceiling joists or rafter ties connecting the rafters. What is the likely cause?
- Hail damage that has cracked the roof sheathing
* Rafter thrust spreading the walls
- Normal settling of a newly built roof
- Too much attic ventilation drying out the rafters
> Without rafter ties or a structural ridge beam, rafters push the walls outward and the ridge drops. This is a significant structural defect that needs an engineer or contractor.

[h119] structure
Per the IRC, when the roof slope is less than 3:12, the member at the ridge that supports the rafters must be:
- A 1-inch ridge board
- A collar tie
* Designed as a beam
- Omitted
> Per the IRC (R802.3), where the roof pitch is less than 3:12, the members that support rafters (such as the ridge) must be designed as beams, because the rafters create little thrust that ceiling joists can resist.

[h120] structure
Per the IRC, in a crawl space, wood joists closer than how far to exposed ground must be naturally durable or preservative-treated?
- 6 inches
- 12 inches
* 18 inches
- 24 inches
> Per the IRC (R317.1), wood joists or the bottom of a wood structural floor less than 18 inches from exposed ground, and wood girders less than 12 inches from exposed ground, must be decay-resistant.

[h121] structure
Per the IRC, wood girders in a crawl space closer than how far to exposed ground must be naturally durable or preservative-treated?
- 6 inches
* 12 inches
- 18 inches
- 24 inches
> Per the IRC (R317.1), girders closer than 12 inches to exposed ground in a crawl space must be decay-resistant. The limit for joists is 18 inches.

[h122] structure
Per the IRC, what is the minimum size of an access opening through a floor into a crawl space?
- 12 by 18 inches
* 18 by 24 inches
- 22 by 30 inches
- 24 by 36 inches
> Per the IRC (R408.4), a crawl space access opening through the floor must be at least 18 by 24 inches; an opening through a perimeter wall must be at least 16 by 24 inches.

[h123] structure
Per the IRC, for a vented crawl space with no ground vapor retarder, what is the minimum net vent area?
- 1 square foot for each 50 square feet of crawl space
* 1 square foot for each 150 square feet of crawl space
- 1 square foot for each 300 square feet of crawl space
- 1 square foot for each 1,500 square feet of crawl space

* Extending the discharge away from the foundation
- Turning the pump off so that it does not run so often
- Adding a second pump in the same pit beside the first one
> Water discharged at the foundation can re-enter the soil around the basement and cycle back into the sump. The discharge should carry the water well away from the house.

[h191] plumbing
What is a common reason old cast iron drain pipes fail?
- They soften and melt during hot summer weather
* Internal corrosion and cracking
- They expand and split when hot water flows through them
- They attract termites, which eat the joints between pipes
> Cast iron corrodes from the inside, and horizontal runs can crack or rot along the bottom where water flows. Stains, rust scale or leaks should be reported for evaluation.

[h192] plumbing
Water is dripping from the ceiling below a second-floor shower, but only while the shower is in use and the curtain is closed. What is the most likely source?
- A leak in the supply line that runs constantly
* A leaking shower pan, drain or enclosure
- Condensation from the attic
- A roof leak
> A leak that appears only during shower use points to the shower pan, drain connection or enclosure (tile, grout or door seals). A supply leak would usually be continuous.

[h193] plumbing
Supply pipes in an unheated crawl space have no insulation or heat tape in a cold-climate house. What is the risk?
- The pipes will sweat in winter
* The pipes can freeze and burst
- The water will taste bad
- The pipes will vibrate
> Water pipes in unheated spaces in cold climates are exposed to freezing. Insulating, heat tracing or relocating them reduces the risk.

[h194] plumbing
The inspector hears a loud bang in the pipes each time a faucet or washing machine valve shuts off quickly. What is this condition called?
* Water hammer
- Cross-connection
- Back-siphonage
- Trap siphonage
> Water hammer is a pressure surge when flowing water stops suddenly. It can damage pipes and fittings. High pressure makes it worse; water hammer arrestors or a pressure-reducing valve can help.

[h195] plumbing
Under typical standards of practice, what must an inspector describe about the water heater?
- Its remaining life in years, based on the serial number
* Its energy source
- Its purchase price and the cost of a replacement unit
- Its manufacturer's warranty terms and registration status
> Standards such as InterNACHI's call for describing the water heating equipment, including the energy source, and inspecting its venting and relief valve. Predicting remaining life is not required.

[h196] plumbing
A gas water heater has rust and scorch marks around the burner access opening, and the inspector sees flames spilling out of the combustion chamber when the burner fires. What is the most appropriate action?
- Adjust the gas valve to reduce the flame size and continue the inspection
* Report it as a hazard needing prompt evaluation by a gas technician
- Report it as normal for gas water heaters that are more than a few years old
- Recommend cleaning the scorched area with a wire brush and repainting it
> Flame rollout and scorching suggest a blocked flue or combustion problem that can cause fire or carbon monoxide hazards. It needs prompt evaluation by a qualified technician.

[h197] plumbing
Shortly after a gas water heater fires, the inspector holds a mirror at the draft hood and it fogs up, and the top of the tank shows heat damage. What does this indicate?
- Normal operation for an atmospheric water heater on startup
* Flue gases spilling at the draft hood (backdrafting)
- The burner is too small for the size of the tank
- The water temperature is set too low on the gas valve
> Fogging and heat damage at the draft hood show that flue gases are spilling into the room, which can expose occupants to carbon monoxide. A qualified technician should evaluate the venting.

[h198] plumbing
A basement bathroom is below the level of the sewer line. What does it usually need?
- An S-trap on each fixture to hold back sewer gas
* A sewage ejector pump in a sealed, vented basin
- A larger water heater to serve the lower level
- A pressure-reducing valve on the water service
> Fixtures below the sewer need a sewage ejector (a pump in a sealed basin with its own vent) to lift waste up to the building drain. A sump pump is for groundwater, not sewage.

[h199] plumbing
Under typical standards of practice, which of these does the inspector inspect in the plumbing system?
* Drain, waste and vent piping that is visible
- Underground sewer laterals
- Interior of water supply pipes inside walls
- Fire sprinkler systems
> Inspectors inspect the visible water supply and drain, waste and vent systems. Underground and concealed piping and fire sprinkler systems are excluded.

[h200] plumbing
A clothes washer standpipe is installed inside a laundry room wall. Per the IRC, the standpipe should extend at least ___ and no more than ___ above its trap weir.
- 6 inches; 18 inches
* 18 inches; 42 inches
- 30 inches; 60 inches
- 42 inches; 72 inches
> Per the IRC (P2706.1.2), a standpipe must extend at least 18 inches and no more than 42 inches above the trap weir. A short standpipe can overflow; a tall one can siphon the trap.

- 15 feet
* 18 feet

* 3 feet
- 6 feet
- 10 feet

[h204] electrical
What is the purpose of the drip loop in the service-drop conductors at the service head?
* To make water run off the low point of the loop instead of into the service head
- To give the utility extra wire for future upgrades
- To reduce the voltage
- To ground the service
> The conductors dip below the service head so that rain runs down to the low point and drips off, rather than following the wires into the conduit and panel.

[h205] electrical
The inspector notices rust and water stains inside the main panel, and the service head on the mast above is missing its cap. What is the most likely cause?
- Condensation from the air conditioner
* Water entering through the damaged service head and running down the service conduit
- A plumbing leak in the attic
- Normal for older panels
> A damaged or missing weatherhead lets rain into the service raceway, which carries water into the panel. Water in a panel is a shock and fire hazard that needs an electrician.

[h206] electrical
Per the IRC, what is the minimum depth of working space in front of a residential electrical panel?
- 24 inches
- 30 inches
* 36 inches
- 48 inches
> Per the IRC (E3405.2), the working space in front of panels must be at least 36 inches deep, at least 30 inches wide (or the width of the equipment) and at least 6-1/2 feet high.

[h207] electrical
Per the IRC, what is the minimum width of the working space in front of an electrical panel?
- 24 inches
* 30 inches, or the width of the equipment if greater
- 36 inches
- 48 inches
> Per the IRC (E3405.2), the work space must be at least 30 inches wide or the width of the equipment, whichever is greater, with doors able to open at least 90 degrees.

[h208] electrical
Per the IRC, an electrical panel containing overcurrent devices must NOT be located in which of these places?
- A garage
- A basement
- A utility room
* A clothes closet or bathroom
> Per the IRC (E3705.7), overcurrent devices must not be located in clothes closets, in bathrooms or over the steps of a stairway.

[h209] electrical
In a panel, the inspector finds two wires connected under a single breaker terminal screw. The breaker is not marked for two conductors. What is this condition, and what is the concern?
- A split bus; normal
* A double tap; the terminal may not clamp both wires securely, which can cause overheating
- A bootleg ground; reverse polarity
- A multiwire branch circuit; required by code
> Most breaker terminals are listed for one conductor. Two wires under one screw (a double tap) can loosen and overheat. A few breakers are listed for two conductors, so the inspector checks the labeling and recommends correction by an electrician if not listed.

[h210] electrical
In a main panel, two white neutral wires are found under one terminal screw on the neutral bar. Why is this a concern?
- It is required in main panels
* Each neutral should have its own terminal so that one circuit can be serviced without disturbing the other and connections stay tight
- It causes reverse polarity
- It makes the breakers trip too often
> The NEC (408.41) requires each grounded (neutral) conductor to end in its own terminal. Shared terminals can loosen, and disconnecting one neutral could interrupt another circuit's neutral.

[h211] electrical
In a subpanel (not the service equipment), the neutral bar is bonded to the metal enclosure, and the grounds and neutrals are mixed on the same bar. What should be reported?
- Nothing, because that is how all panels are wired
* The neutrals and equipment grounds should be separated, with the neutral bar isolated from the enclosure
- The subpanel needs a larger main breaker
- The subpanel should be moved outdoors
> Neutrals and grounds are bonded together only at the service equipment. In a subpanel, a bonded neutral puts normal current on the grounding path and metal parts, so they must be separated (a four-wire feeder).

[h212] electrical
Where is the main bonding jumper that connects the neutral to the equipment grounding system supposed to be?
* At the service equipment only
- In every subpanel
- At every receptacle
- At the meter only
> The neutral and grounding systems are bonded only at the service (the main disconnect). Bonding elsewhere creates parallel paths for neutral current.

[h213] electrical
Per the IRC, a single ground rod electrode must be at least how long?
- 4 feet
- 6 feet
* 8 feet
- 10 feet
> Per the IRC (E3608.1.4), rod electrodes must be at least 8 feet long and driven so that at least 8 feet is in contact with soil. Rods of 5/8 inch diameter, or 1/2 inch if listed, are typical.

[h214] electrical
Per the IRC, when two ground rods are used, how far apart must they be?
- 2 feet
* 6 feet
- 10 feet
- 25 feet
> Per the IRC (E3608.4 and E3608.1.4), a single rod with resistance over 25 ohms needs a supplemental electrode. Where more than one rod is used, they must be at least 6 feet apart.

[h215] electrical
Why must a metal underground water pipe used as a grounding electrode be supplemented by an additional electrode?
- Water pipes conduct electricity too well
* The pipe may later be replaced with plastic or isolated, so the grounding would be lost
- Water pipes cannot be bonded
- Supplementing is optional
> Per the IRC (E3608.1.1), a metal underground water pipe electrode must be supplemented by another electrode, because future plastic repairs could break the path to earth.

[h216] electrical
A 20-amp breaker protects a circuit wired with 14 AWG copper. What is the concern?
- None, because 14 AWG is rated for 20 amps
* The wire is too small for the breaker, so it can overheat before the breaker trips
- The breaker is too small for the wire
- The circuit will have reverse polarity
> 14 AWG copper is limited to 15 amps of overcurrent protection. A 20-amp breaker on 14 AWG wire (overfusing) is a fire hazard and should be corrected by an electrician.

[h217] electrical
Under the general rule, what is the maximum overcurrent protection for 12 AWG copper branch-circuit wire?
- 15 amps
* 20 amps
- 30 amps
- 40 amps
> Under the small-conductor rule in the NEC (240.4(D)) and the IRC, copper wire is limited to 15 amps for 14 AWG, 20 amps for 12 AWG and 30 amps for 10 AWG.

[h218] electrical
A 30-amp double-pole breaker serves an electric water heater. What is the smallest copper wire normally allowed on it?
- 14 AWG
- 12 AWG
* 10 AWG
- 6 AWG
> Under the general rule, 10 AWG copper may be protected at up to 30 amps. Smaller wire on a 30-amp breaker would be overfused.

[h219] electrical
Solid-conductor aluminum branch-circuit wiring was commonly installed in homes from about the mid-1960s to the mid-1970s. What is the main concern?
- It cannot carry 120 volts
* Connections can loosen and overheat, creating a fire risk, especially at devices not rated for aluminum
- It always has reverse polarity
- It attracts lightning
> Single-strand aluminum branch wiring expands, oxidizes and creeps at terminals, which can lead to overheating. The inspector should report it and recommend evaluation by an electrician familiar with approved repair methods.

[h220] electrical
Which of these is a widely recognized repair method for solid-conductor aluminum branch wiring?
- Wrapping each connection in electrical tape
- Replacing breakers with larger ones
* Pigtailing to copper with special connectors listed for the purpose, such as the COPALUM crimp or AlumiConn
- Painting the wires with anti-rust paint
> The CPSC recognizes copper pigtailing using the COPALUM crimp or AlumiConn connectors as repair methods. Ordinary twist-on connectors are not a recommended repair.

[h221] electrical
The service-entrance conductors are multi-strand aluminum. How should this generally be reported?
* As a common, acceptable installation when properly installed and terminated
- As a fire hazard that must be replaced with copper
- As the same hazard as solid aluminum branch wiring
- As a sign of an illegal service
> Stranded aluminum service and feeder conductors are common and acceptable. The known problems apply to small-gauge solid aluminum branch wiring. Terminations should be tight, often with antioxidant compound.

[h222] electrical
An inspector identifies a Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) panel with Stab-Lok breakers. What is the most appropriate report comment?
- No comment, because brand names are not reported
* Note that these breakers have a history of failing to trip under overload, and recommend evaluation by a licensed electrician
- State that the panel will catch fire within a year
- Recommend replacing the breakers with larger ones
> FPE Stab-Lok breakers have a widely reported history of failing to trip. The inspector should report the panel type and the concern and refer it to an electrician, without predicting exact outcomes.

[h223] electrical
Zinsco (and some Sylvania-Zinsco) panels are reported as a concern mainly because:
- They have too many breaker spaces
* Breakers can overheat and melt or fuse to the bus bar, so they may not trip
- They are only rated for 30 amps
- They are always installed outdoors
> Zinsco breakers are known for loose connections that overheat and can weld to the aluminum bus, defeating protection. The panel should be evaluated by an electrician.

[h224] electrical
In a fuse panel, a 30-amp fuse is installed on a circuit wired with 14 AWG copper. What is the concern, and what device helps prevent it?
- No concern; a bigger fuse is safer
* Overfusing; Type S fuse adapters limit the size of fuse that can be installed
- Reverse polarity; a GFCI fixes it
- Undervoltage; a larger wire fixes it
> A 30-amp fuse on 14 AWG wire lets the wire overheat. Type S (non-tamperable) fuses and adapters keep a larger fuse from being screwed into a smaller circuit.

[h225] electrical
Knob-and-tube wiring is found buried under loose-fill insulation in an attic. What is the concern?
- None, because insulation protects the wires
* The wiring was designed to release heat into open air, and burying it can cause overheating; it also has no equipment ground
- The wiring will cause the insulation to get wet
- The wiring is always aluminum
> Knob-and-tube depends on open air for cooling, and the NEC (394.12) prohibits its use in hollow spaces with insulation that envelops the conductors. Its age, brittle insulation and lack of a ground also call for evaluation by an electrician.

[h226] electrical
A plug-in receptacle tester shows "open ground" at a three-prong receptacle. What does this mean?
- The hot and neutral are reversed
* The receptacle's equipment ground is not connected
- The circuit has no power
- The receptacle is GFCI-protected
> An open ground means the grounding terminal has no path to ground, so the third prong gives a false sense of protection. It should be corrected or, where allowed, protected with a GFCI and labeled.

[h227] electrical
A receptacle tester shows "hot/neutral reversed" at a receptacle. What is the hazard?
* The fixture's shell or a device's switch may be energized even when the device is switched off
- The circuit breaker will be too large
- The receptacle will not work
- The wiring will turn to aluminum
> Reverse polarity puts the hot conductor on the neutral side, so lamp shells and some appliance parts can remain energized when switched off. It should be corrected by an electrician.

[h228] electrical
Why can a "bootleg ground" fool a standard three-light receptacle tester?
- It makes the tester's lights flicker
* The neutral is jumpered to the ground screw, so the tester sees a correct wiring pattern even though there is no true ground
- It removes power from the circuit
- It reverses the hot and neutral
> A bootleg ground connects the grounding terminal to the neutral at the receptacle. A basic tester reads it as correct, but the metal parts can become energized if the neutral fails. Grounding receptacles on older ungrounded wiring should raise suspicion.

[h229] electrical
An older house has two-prong receptacles on ungrounded wiring. Under the NEC, which is an acceptable way to replace a two-prong receptacle with a three-prong one?
- Install a three-prong receptacle with nothing connected to the ground screw, without any label
* Protect the receptacle with a GFCI and label it "No Equipment Ground"
- Connect the ground screw to the neutral screw
- Connect the ground screw to a water pipe in the wall
> The NEC (406.4(D)) allows a three-prong receptacle on an ungrounded circuit if it is GFCI-protected and marked "No Equipment Ground" (or the grounding conductor is properly added).

[h230] electrical
What is the main purpose of a GFCI (ground-fault circuit interrupter)?
- To prevent wiring fires caused by arcing
* To protect people from electric shock by cutting power when current leaks to ground
- To prevent overloads on the circuit
- To raise the voltage on long circuits
> A GFCI compares current on the hot and neutral and trips when about 4 to 6 milliamps leaks to ground, protecting people from shock. AFCIs address arcing; breakers protect wiring from overloads.

[h231] electrical
What is the main purpose of an AFCI (arc-fault circuit interrupter)?
* To reduce the risk of fire by detecting dangerous arcing in the wiring
- To protect people from shock in wet locations
- To prevent power surges from lightning
- To reduce the electric bill
> AFCIs detect the electrical signature of arcing faults, such as damaged cords or loose connections, and open the circuit to prevent fires. They are not a substitute for GFCIs in wet locations.

[h232] electrical
Per the IRC, which location does NOT generally require GFCI protection for 125-volt, 15- and 20-amp receptacles?
- A bathroom
- A garage
- An outdoor receptacle
* A bedroom
> Per the IRC (E3902), GFCI protection is required in bathrooms, garages, outdoors, crawl spaces, unfinished basements, kitchen countertop areas, within 6 feet of sinks and in laundry areas. Bedrooms require AFCI protection, not GFCI.

[h233] electrical
Per the IRC, which kitchen receptacles require GFCI protection?

* All receptacles serving the countertop surfaces
- Only receptacles behind the refrigerator
- None, if the kitchen has a window
> Per the IRC (E3902), all 125-volt, 15- and 20-amp receptacles that serve kitchen countertop surfaces require GFCI protection.

[h234] electrical
Per the IRC, which of these locations requires AFCI protection for 120-volt, 15- and 20-amp branch circuits?
- An outdoor receptacle
- A crawl space
* A hallway
- A bathroom
> Per the IRC (E3902), AFCI protection is required for circuits supplying outlets in kitchens, family rooms, dining rooms, living rooms, parlors, libraries, dens, bedrooms, sunrooms, recreation rooms, closets, hallways, laundry areas and similar rooms.

[h235] electrical

* 36 inches

[h239] electrical

[h240] electrical
In an attic, the inspector finds several wire splices wrapped in tape and lying on top of the insulation, not inside any box. What should the inspector report?

- It is a cosmetic issue

[h241] electrical

- It allows too much light into the panel

[h242] electrical
The dead-front cover of a panel is held on with long, pointed sheet-metal screws. Why is this a concern?

- They rust faster
- They make the panel look unprofessional only

[h243] electrical
What does a melted or discolored wire insulation and scorch marks on a breaker indicate?
* Overheating, such as from a loose connection or overload
- Normal aging
- A properly sized breaker
- Too much insulation in the attic
> Discoloration and melting show the connection or conductor has overheated. That is a fire hazard needing prompt evaluation by an electrician.

[h244] electrical
Before removing the dead-front cover of an electrical panel, what is a sensible first step?

- Pry the cover off from the bottom
* Check the cover and surroundings for hazards, such as water, rust, scorching or energized metal (for example with a non-contact voltage tester), and stand safely
- Ask the homeowner to remove it

[h245] electrical

[h246] electrical

[h247] electrical

- Green
* White or gray
- Red
- Black

[h248] electrical

[h249] electrical

* A representative number
- Only the ones the client points out

[h250] electrical

# ---------------- Heating & Cooling (h251-h300) ----------------

[h251] hvac
A central air conditioner has run for 15 minutes. The air at the supply register is only 5 degrees F cooler than the return air. How should this be reported?
- As normal, because any temperature drop is acceptable
* As below the commonly expected range of roughly 15 to 20 degrees F, with evaluation recommended by an HVAC technician
- As a sign that the system is oversized
- As an electrical defect
> A temperature split well below the typical range suggests low refrigerant, airflow problems or a failing compressor. The split is a screening tool, so the inspector refers the system rather than diagnosing it.

[h252] hvac
The outdoor temperature is 50 degrees F on inspection day. What should the inspector generally do about testing the central air conditioner?
- Run it for at least an hour to be sure it works
* Not operate it, because running it in low outdoor temperatures can damage the compressor, and report that it was not tested
- Run it with the outdoor unit covered
- Bypass the low-temperature lockout
> Manufacturers typically warn against operating cooling below about 60 to 65 degrees F. Standards of practice do not require operating equipment when conditions could damage it; the limitation is reported.

[h253] hvac
In winter, the outdoor unit of a heat pump stops its fan and releases a cloud of steam for a few minutes. What is most likely happening?
- The compressor is on fire
* The unit is in a normal defrost cycle, melting frost off the outdoor coil
- The refrigerant is leaking
- The unit is overheating
> Heat pumps periodically reverse to warm the outdoor coil and melt frost. Steam and a stopped fan during this cycle are normal; a coil that stays encased in ice is not.

[h254] hvac
On a heat pump thermostat, what does "emergency heat" or "auxiliary heat" usually refer to?
* Backup heating, often electric resistance strips, used when the heat pump cannot keep up or has failed
- A second outdoor unit
- A gas fireplace connected to the thermostat
- Turning the system off in an emergency
> Heat pumps use supplemental heat (usually electric strips) in very cold weather, during defrost, or when "emergency heat" is selected because the compressor has failed.

[h255] hvac
When a gas furnace's blower starts, the burner flames waver and lean noticeably. What may this indicate?
- Normal operation
* A possible breach in the heat exchanger letting blower air disturb the flames
- A dirty air filter only
- An oversized gas line
> Flame movement when the blower starts can indicate a cracked heat exchanger. The inspector should report it and recommend evaluation by a qualified HVAC technician, because of the carbon monoxide risk.

[h256] hvac
Under typical standards of practice, is the home inspector required to inspect the interior of a furnace's heat exchanger?
- Yes, always
* No; it is not readily accessible and requires dismantling, which is beyond a home inspection
- Only on furnaces less than 10 years old
- Only if the client asks
> Standards such as ASHI and InterNACHI exclude inspecting heat exchanger interiors. Inspectors report visible signs that suggest a problem and refer to an HVAC technician.

[h257] hvac
Scorch marks and melted wire insulation are found just outside the burner compartment of a gas furnace. What condition do these most likely indicate?
* Flame rollout, often from a blocked flue or heat exchanger problem
- Normal wear
- An oversized air filter
- A failed thermostat

# ---------------- Reporting & Professional Practice (h351-h400) ----------------

[h351] practice
Under typical home inspection standards of practice, a home inspection is best described as:
- A technically exhaustive engineering evaluation of every system
- A guarantee that the house has no hidden defects
* A non-invasive visual examination of readily accessible systems and components, reported as of the time of inspection
- A code compliance inspection for the local building department
> Standards such as ASHI's and InterNACHI's define a home inspection as a non-invasive, visual examination of readily accessible components. It is not technically exhaustive and does not guarantee against concealed defects.

[h352] practice
Under ASHI's definitions, "readily accessible" means available for visual inspection:
* Without moving personal property, dismantling, destructive measures or actions likely to involve risk to persons or property
- After moving any furniture the client requests
- Only with the help of a ladder longer than 20 feet
- Only after removing finished wall surfaces
> Inspectors are not required to move belongings, dismantle components or take risky or destructive actions. Areas that are blocked or unsafe are reported as not inspected.

[h353] practice
Under ASHI's standard of practice, a "representative number" of multiple similar interior components, such as windows and receptacles, means:
- Every one of them
- Half of them
* One component per room
- Only those the seller points out
> ASHI defines a representative number as one component per room for multiple similar interior components and one on each side of the building for multiple similar exterior components.

[h354] practice
Under typical standards of practice, when the inspector does not inspect a system or component that is normally included, what must the report state?
- Nothing, because the client will assume it was skipped
* That it was not inspected and the reason it was not inspected
- That it is in good condition
- That the seller is responsible for it
> Standards of practice require reporting systems and components that were not inspected and why, such as being inaccessible, shut down or unsafe to reach.

[h355] practice
The water is shut off at the main valve in a vacant, bank-owned house. What is the most appropriate action?
- Turn the main valve on and inspect the plumbing
- Report that the plumbing system is in good condition
* Leave the valve closed, report that the plumbing could not be fully tested and why, and recommend inspection once the water is restored
- Refund the inspection fee
> Standards of practice do not require operating shut-off valves or systems that are shut down. Turning on water in a winterized or neglected house can cause flooding, so the limitation is reported instead.

[h356] practice
Under typical standards of practice, which is the inspector NOT required to determine?
- Whether the water heater has a TPR valve
* Whether the house complies with current building codes
- Whether visible guards have wide openings
- Whether the furnace responds to normal controls
> Home inspections are not code inspections. Inspectors may use codes as a reference for safety, but determining code compliance is excluded from standards of practice.

[h357] practice
A buyer asks the inspector how much it will cost to replace the roof. Under typical standards of practice, what is the most appropriate response?
- Give an exact price
* Explain that cost estimates are outside the inspection and suggest getting quotes from qualified roofers
- Refuse to discuss the roof at all
- Tell the buyer to ask the seller for a credit of a specific amount
> Standards of practice exclude determining the cost of repairs. Inspectors identify and report conditions; contractors provide estimates.

[h358] practice
At the end of the inspection, the buyer asks, "Should I buy this house?" What is the most appropriate response?
- "Yes, it's a great house"
- "No, walk away"
* Explain that advising on whether to buy is outside the inspector's role, and help the client understand the findings so they can decide
- Recommend a lower purchase price
> Standards of practice exclude advising on the advisability of purchase and market value. The inspector's job is to explain the findings clearly.

[h359] practice
Under both the ASHI and InterNACHI codes of ethics, an inspector must not perform or offer to perform repairs on a home they inspected, for compensation, for how long after the inspection?
- 30 days
- 90 days
* 12 months (one year)
- Five years
> Both codes of ethics prohibit performing or offering repairs on systems or components covered by the inspection for 12 months (one year) after the inspection, to avoid a conflict of interest.

[h360] practice
A real estate agent offers an inspector $50 for every client the agent refers. What is the most appropriate response under typical codes of ethics?
- Accept, because referral fees are standard
* Decline, because accepting compensation from parties with an interest in the sale is a conflict of interest
- Accept, but only for high-priced homes
- Accept the money and not tell the client
> Codes of ethics prohibit accepting commissions, fees or other compensation from real estate agents or other parties with an interest in the transaction, because it can bias the report.

[h361] practice
An inspector's brother-in-law is the listing agent for a house a client wants inspected. What should the inspector do?
- Inspect without mentioning it
* Disclose the relationship to the client before accepting the inspection, so the client can decide
- Give the brother-in-law a copy of the report first
- Refuse to inspect any house in the city
> Codes of ethics require inspectors to disclose any conflict of interest, such as a family or financial relationship with a party to the transaction, before the inspection.

[h362] practice
The seller asks the inspector for a copy of the buyer's inspection report. What should the inspector do?
- Send it immediately, because the seller owns the house
* Provide it only with the client's permission, unless the law requires otherwise
- Send the seller a summary but not the full report
- Post the report online
> The report belongs to the client. Codes of ethics prohibit disclosing inspection results without the client's approval, except where required by law or for an immediate safety hazard.

[h363] practice
Under ASHI's code of ethics, when may an inspector disclose an observed immediate safety hazard to someone other than the client?
* To occupants who are exposed to the hazard, when feasible
- Never, under any circumstances
- Only after the closing
- Only to the real estate agent
> ASHI's code allows inspectors, at their discretion, to disclose observed immediate safety hazards to occupants exposed to them, when feasible.

[h364] practice
After the inspection, the listing agent asks the inspector to delete a comment about a missing GFCI because it might "upset the buyer." What should the inspector do?
- Delete it to keep the agent happy
- Change the wording to say it is optional
* Refuse, because the report is for the client and must reflect the inspector's honest findings
- Remove it only from the summary page
> The inspector's duty is to the client. Altering findings at the request of another party would be unethical. Corrections are made only for genuine factual errors.

[h365] practice
Which report comment is the best example of clear, useful report writing?
- "Electrical issues noted."
- "Monitor the roof."
* "The upstairs hall bathroom receptacle did not trip when tested and is not GFCI-protected. This is a shock hazard. Recommend correction by a licensed electrician."
- "Some things might need work; consult a pro."
> A good comment states what was found, where it is, why it matters and what to do and by whom. Vague phrases like "issues noted" or "monitor" leave the client without useful guidance.

[h366] practice
What is the main problem with ending a report comment with "monitor" for a structural crack?
- It is too specific
* It shifts responsibility to the client without guidance, and the client may not know what to watch or when to act
- It is required by all standards
- It makes the report too long
> "Monitor" alone gives no criteria. If the condition matters, the better approach is to describe it and recommend evaluation by a qualified professional.

[h367] practice
The inspector sees stains and soft drywall under a window but is not sure of the cause. What is the best reporting approach?
- Guess the cause and state it as fact
- Leave it out because the cause is unknown
* Describe what was observed and where, explain the possible significance, and recommend further evaluation
- Tell the client it is probably nothing
> Inspectors are not required to determine causes, but they must report significant findings. Describing the observation and recommending evaluation is accurate and defensible.

[h368] practice
Under ASHI's definitions, "further evaluation" means:
- Asking the inspector to return the next day
* Examination and analysis by a qualified professional, tradesman or service technician beyond what the home inspection provides
- The client looking at the item again
- A second inspection by the same inspector at no cost
> Recommending further evaluation tells the client a specialist is needed to diagnose the condition, its cause and the repair.

[h369] practice
When should the pre-inspection agreement (contract) ideally be signed?
- After the report is delivered
- At the closing
* Before the inspection begins
- Only if there is a dispute
> A signed pre-inspection agreement defines the scope, limitations, fee and terms before work starts, which protects both the client and the inspector.

[h370] practice
What does a pre-inspection agreement typically include?
- A guarantee that the house has no defects
* The scope of the inspection, exclusions and limitations, the fee, and terms such as dispute resolution
- The repair price for each defect
- The appraised value of the home
> The agreement sets expectations by defining what is and is not included, referencing the standard of practice and stating business terms.

[h371] practice
In a negligence claim against a home inspector, which four elements generally must the client prove?
- Fraud, intent, malice and damages
* Duty, breach of duty, causation and damages
- Price, location, timing and age of the house
- Contract, signature, payment and receipt
> Negligence requires a duty owed, a breach of the standard of care, a causal link between the breach and the harm, and actual damages.

[h372] practice
What type of insurance mainly covers claims that the inspector missed or misreported a defect?
- General liability insurance
* Errors and omissions (professional liability) insurance
- Workers' compensation insurance
- Auto insurance
> E&O insurance covers claims of professional negligence, such as missed defects. General liability covers bodily injury and property damage, such as breaking a window during the inspection.

[h373] practice
While opening an attic hatch, the inspector accidentally cracks a ceiling light fixture. Which insurance would typically cover this?
* General liability insurance
- Errors and omissions insurance
- Health insurance
- Title insurance
> General liability insurance covers physical damage to property and bodily injury that happen during the inspector's work. E&O covers errors in professional judgment and reporting.

[h374] practice
Why do inspectors typically include a limitation of liability clause in their agreement?
- To avoid any duty to the client
* To limit the inspector's financial exposure, often to the inspection fee, where state law allows such clauses
- To guarantee the client full replacement cost for any missed defect
- To prevent the client from reading the report
> Limitation of liability clauses try to cap damages. Their enforceability varies by state, and some states restrict or prohibit them for home inspectors.

[h375] practice
What should an inspector record about conditions on the day of inspection?
- Only the client's name
* Weather and other conditions that limited the inspection, such as rain, snow cover, outdoor temperature or utilities that were off
- The seller's reasons for selling
- The real estate commission
> Conditions affect what can be seen and tested. Recording them explains limitations, such as not testing air conditioning in cold weather or not seeing a snow-covered roof.

[h376] practice
The house is full of stored boxes that block access to the basement walls and the electrical panel. What should the inspector do?
- Move all the boxes and inspect behind them
* Inspect what is accessible, and report the areas that were blocked and that defects could be hidden there
- Report the house as having no basement defects
- Cancel the inspection without explanation
> Standards of practice do not require moving personal property. The report should clearly state the limitations so that the client can arrange access if needed.

[h377] practice
Per OSHA guidance, an extension ladder should be set up so that its base is about how far from the wall for every 4 feet of ladder height to the support point?
- 6 inches
* 1 foot
- 2 feet
- 4 feet
> The 4-to-1 rule puts the base 1 foot out for every 4 feet of working height. Ladders used to reach a roof should also extend at least 3 feet above the roof edge.

[h378] practice
When a ladder is used to access a roof, how far should it extend above the roof edge?
- 1 foot
* 3 feet
- 5 feet
- It should stop just below the gutter
> Extending the ladder at least 3 feet above the landing surface gives a handhold for getting on and off the roof safely, per OSHA guidance.

[h379] practice
An aggressive dog is loose in the crawl space access area. What should the inspector do?

* Not enter the area, report that it was not inspected because of the unsafe condition, and offer to return if the situation changes
- Use the dog as an excuse to skip the whole inspection
- Ask the client to remove the dog
> Inspectors are not required to enter areas that are unsafe. The limitation is reported, and a return visit can be arranged.

[h380] practice
Which of these is a sound reason to include photographs in an inspection report?
* To help the client understand the location and nature of findings
- To replace written descriptions entirely
- To show the seller's belongings
- To make the report longer
> Photos support but do not replace written descriptions. They help clients and contractors find and understand the conditions.

[h381] practice
A client asks the inspector to test the home for radon as part of the inspection. What is the appropriate approach?

* Radon testing is an ancillary service outside the standard of practice; the inspector may offer it if properly trained, and certified or licensed where required
- Tell the client that radon is not a real concern
- Estimate the radon level by looking at the basement
> Environmental testing such as radon is excluded from standards of practice but may be offered as an added service with proper training and any required credentials, using approved test protocols.

[h382] practice
Under typical standards of practice, the inspector is NOT required to report on:
- Safety hazards observed
- Components not functioning properly
* The remaining service life of systems and components
- Systems that were not inspected and why
> Inspectors report deficiencies and safety issues but are not required to predict remaining life. They may note when a system appears near the end of its typical service life.

[h383] practice
During the walkthrough, the inspector tells the client the roof "looks fine," but the written report later describes significant roof damage. What is the main risk?
- None, because only the verbal comments count
* Conflicting statements can confuse the client and create liability; verbal and written findings should agree
- The client will read the report faster
- The report will be shorter
> Inconsistent communication undermines trust and can lead to claims. Inspectors should be careful that on-site comments match the written report.

[h384] practice
What is a key reason inspectors should avoid citing specific code sections as requirements in their reports?
- Codes do not exist for homes
* Home inspections are not code inspections; the code that applied when the house was built may differ, and the jurisdiction decides compliance
- Clients prefer longer explanations
- Codes are secret
> Inspectors can explain why a condition is unsafe or improper, sometimes referencing generally accepted practice, but they do not determine code compliance.

[h385] practice
A newly built home is being inspected before closing. The builder says the city already inspected it, so the inspector should skip the attic. What should the inspector do?

* Inspect the attic as agreed with the client, because municipal inspections do not replace the client's home inspection
- Ask the builder to inspect the attic instead
- Report the attic as acceptable without looking
> The inspector works for the client and follows the agreed scope. Code inspections check different things, and new homes can have significant defects.

[h386] practice
Which statement about InterNACHI's definition of a "material defect" is most accurate?

* A specific issue that may significantly affect the value of the property or that poses an unreasonable risk to people
- Only defects that cost more than $10,000
- Any item the seller has disclosed
> InterNACHI defines a material defect as a specific issue with a system or component that may have a significant, adverse impact on the property's value, or that poses an unreasonable risk to people. Age or end of service life alone is not necessarily a material defect.

[h387] practice
The client asks the inspector to certify that the house will have no problems for five years. What is the correct response?
- Agree, for an extra fee
* Explain that a home inspection is not a warranty or guarantee and reports conditions only at the time of inspection
- Agree, but only for the roof
- Refuse to perform the inspection
> Standards of practice state the inspection is not a warranty or guarantee. Some inspectors partner with third-party warranty providers, but the inspection itself does not guarantee future performance.

[h388] practice
A probing awl is pushed into a finished, painted wood baseboard that looks sound, just to check. Under ASHI's standard, why is this inappropriate?

* Probing is not required where it would damage a finished surface or where no deterioration is visible or presumed
- Probing must be done only with a moisture meter
- Probing is required on all surfaces
> ASHI calls for probing structural components where deterioration is suspected, but not where probing would damage finished surfaces or where no deterioration is visible or presumed.

[h389] practice
An inspector discovers wiring that is energized and exposed in a reachable location in an occupied home. What is the best course of action?
* Inform the client and, where feasible, the occupants of the immediate hazard, and report it clearly as a safety issue
- Fix the wiring personally
- Keep it secret until the report is delivered
- Turn off the main power to the house and leave
> Immediate hazards should be communicated promptly. Ethics codes allow disclosure to exposed occupants. The inspector should not perform repairs.

[h390] practice
An inspector's fee is set so that it is higher if more defects are found. Why is this a problem?
- It makes the report shorter
* It creates a conflict of interest, because the inspector benefits financially from the findings
- It is required by most standards
- It lowers the inspector's insurance costs
> Compensation that depends on the inspection's findings, or on whether the sale closes, gives the inspector a motive to slant the report. Fees should be independent of the findings.

[h391] practice
Under InterNACHI's standards, which of these is the inspector NOT required to do?

- Describe the type of heating system
- Report as in need of correction any heating system that did not operate
* Move insulation to inspect framing beneath it
> Inspectors are not required to move insulation, personal items or debris that obstruct access. The resulting limitation is reported.

- 50

- A raw score of 150

A client calls a week after the inspection and asks a question about the report. What is the best practice?

- Tell the client to ask the seller

[h396] practice
Which of these best describes the inspector's main duty under typical standards of practice?

* To observe, describe and report on the condition of readily accessible systems and components for the client
- To approve the mortgage loan
- To appraise the home's value
> The inspector's role is to inspect and report objectively for the client. Negotiation, lending and appraisal are other professionals' roles.

[h397] practice
Two days before the inspection, the inspector is asked by a homeowner to pre-inspect the house and then also inspect it for the buyer, for two fees, without telling either party. Why is this improper?
- It is not improper
* Codes of ethics prohibit accepting compensation from more than one party for the same service without the informed consent of all parties
- The inspector must charge both parties the same fee
- The buyer's inspection must happen first
> ASHI's code says inspectors must not accept compensation from more than one party for the same service without the consent of all parties. Undisclosed dual roles create conflicts of interest.

[h398] practice
Which wording best describes a safety concern without overstating it?
- "This will definitely cause a fire."
* "Overheated wiring was observed at the breaker. This is a potential fire hazard; recommend evaluation and repair by a licensed electrician."
- "Wiring seems okay, probably."
- "Electrical system condemned."
> Good reporting states the observation, explains the potential consequence and recommends the right professional, without exaggerated predictions or vague minimization.

[h399] practice
An inspector cannot access the attic because the only hatch is sealed shut with paint and caulk. What is the most appropriate action?
- Cut the hatch open with a utility knife
* Report that the attic was not inspected because the access was sealed, and recommend inspection once access is available
- Assume the attic is in good condition
- Inspect the attic from the roof vents
> Breaking seals or damaging finishes is destructive and outside the standard. Reporting the limitation and recommending access is the correct approach.

[h400] practice
Why is it important for a report to separate safety hazards and significant defects from minor maintenance items?
- So that the report can skip minor items
* So that the client can quickly see the conditions that most affect safety and the decision to buy
- Because standards require only safety items
- So that the seller pays for maintenance items
> Organizing findings by significance helps the client focus on what matters most, while the full report still lists the minor items.
