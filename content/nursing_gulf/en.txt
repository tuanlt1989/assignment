# Gulf nursing licensure (DHA / DOH / MOH / SCFHS / QCHP Prometric-style) practice bank.
# Original items covering general registered-nurse knowledge. Not official exam content.

[g001] fundamentals
What is the single most effective way to prevent healthcare-associated infections?
- Wearing gloves for every patient contact
* Performing hand hygiene at the right moments
- Giving prophylactic antibiotics
- Keeping the patient in a private room
> Hand hygiene (WHO "5 moments") is the most effective measure. Gloves never replace hand hygiene.

[g002] fundamentals
A nurse's hands are visibly soiled with body fluids. The nurse should:
- Use an alcohol-based hand rub
* Wash hands with soap and water
- Put on gloves without cleaning
- Wipe hands with a dry paper towel
> Alcohol rubs do not remove visible dirt or organic material. Wash with soap and water when hands are visibly soiled.

[g003] fundamentals
A patient has Clostridioides difficile diarrhea. Which measures are correct?
* Contact precautions and hand washing with soap and water
- Airborne precautions and alcohol hand rub only
- Droplet precautions only
- Standard precautions only
> C. difficile spores are not reliably killed by alcohol. Use contact precautions, gloves and gown, and wash with soap and water.

[g004] fundamentals
A patient with suspected pulmonary tuberculosis requires:
- A surgical mask for staff and a regular room
* Airborne precautions with an N95 (or higher) respirator and a negative-pressure room
- Contact precautions only
- No special precautions until culture results
> TB spreads by airborne droplet nuclei. Place the patient in an airborne infection isolation room and wear a fit-tested respirator.

[g005] fundamentals
Which infection requires droplet precautions?
- Measles
- Varicella
* Influenza
- Clostridioides difficile
> Influenza, pertussis and meningococcal disease need droplet precautions. Measles and varicella need airborne precautions.

[g006] fundamentals
What is the recommended order for putting on (donning) PPE?
- Gloves, gown, mask, goggles
* Gown, mask or respirator, goggles or face shield, gloves
- Mask, gloves, gown, goggles
- Goggles, gloves, mask, gown
> Gloves go on last so they cover the gown cuffs. When removing PPE, gloves (the most contaminated) usually come off first.

[g007] fundamentals
Before giving a medication, the nurse must identify the patient using:
- The room and bed number
* At least two identifiers, such as full name and date of birth
- The patient's diagnosis
- The name on the door sign
> Two patient identifiers are an international patient safety goal. Room numbers are never used as identifiers.

[g008] fundamentals
Which action best reduces the risk of falls for an older patient at night?
- Raising all four side rails
* Keeping the bed in the lowest position with the call bell within reach
- Giving a sedative at bedtime
- Turning off all lights
> Low bed, call bell, non-slip footwear, night light and regular rounding reduce falls. Four raised rails can increase injury.

[g009] fundamentals
A patient in physical restraints should have restraints released for skin care and range of motion at least every:
- 30 minutes
* 2 hours
- 8 hours
- 24 hours
> Monitor frequently, and release restraints at least every 2 hours (or per policy) to check skin, circulation and offer fluids and toileting.

[g010] fundamentals
A pressure injury with partial-thickness skin loss and exposed pink dermis is classified as:
- Stage 1
* Stage 2
- Stage 3
- Stage 4
> Stage 1 is intact skin with non-blanchable redness. Stage 3 involves full-thickness loss into fat; stage 4 exposes muscle, tendon or bone.

[g011] fundamentals
Which nursing action best prevents pressure injuries in an immobile patient?
- Massaging bony prominences vigorously
* Repositioning at least every 2 hours and using a pressure-redistributing surface
- Keeping the head of the bed at 90 degrees all day
- Using a donut-shaped cushion
> Regular repositioning, skin assessment, nutrition and moisture control prevent pressure injuries. Donut cushions and massage are not recommended.

[g012] fundamentals
When caring for several patients, which need is the nurse's first priority?
* Airway
- Pain
- Anxiety
- Hygiene
> Use the ABC priority framework: airway, breathing, circulation, then other needs.

[g013] fundamentals
The normal resting respiratory rate for an adult is:
- 6 to 10 breaths per minute
* 12 to 20 breaths per minute
- 24 to 30 breaths per minute
- 30 to 40 breaths per minute
> Adults normally breathe 12–20 times a minute. Report persistent rates outside this range in context.

[g014] fundamentals
A fire starts in a patient's room. Following RACE, the nurse's first action is to:
* Rescue patients in immediate danger
- Activate the alarm
- Contain the fire by closing doors
- Extinguish the fire
> RACE = Rescue, Alarm, Contain, Extinguish/Evacuate. To use an extinguisher, remember PASS: Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep.

- Teaching a patient to self-inject insulin
* Measuring vital signs of a stable patient
- Evaluating a patient's response to a new medication
> Assessment, teaching and evaluation stay with the RN. Routine tasks for stable patients may be delegated.

[g018] fundamentals

- Recap the needle using both hands
- Bend the needle before disposal
* Discard the uncapped needle immediately in a puncture-resistant sharps container
- Place the needle in the regular trash
> Never recap used needles with two hands. Immediate disposal prevents needlestick injuries.

[g019] fundamentals

- 80% to 85%
- 85% to 89%
* 95% to 100%
- 100% to 110%
> Targets may be lower for some patients, such as those with chronic CO2 retention (often 88–92%).

[g020] fundamentals

* Explore the reason for refusal, document it and inform the prescriber
- Ask a family member to give it
> Competent adults have the right to refuse treatment. Explore concerns, educate, document and notify.

[g021] medsurg
A diabetic patient is shaky, sweaty and confused, with a blood glucose of 50 mg/dL (2.8 mmol/L). The patient is awake and can swallow. The nurse should first:
* Give 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate, then recheck glucose in 15 minutes
- Give the scheduled insulin dose
- Give a high-protein meal only
- Wait one hour and recheck
> This is the "rule of 15." If the patient cannot swallow, give IV dextrose or glucagon per protocol.

[g022] medsurg
Which finding is typical of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)?
- Bradycardia and hypothermia
* Deep, rapid (Kussmaul) respirations and fruity breath odor
- Blood glucose below 70 mg/dL
- Weight gain and edema
> DKA causes hyperglycemia, ketosis and metabolic acidosis. Treatment includes IV fluids, insulin infusion and potassium monitoring.

[g023] medsurg
Which insulin can be given intravenously?
- Insulin glargine
- NPH insulin
* Regular insulin
- Insulin detemir
> Regular (short-acting) insulin is the type used in IV insulin infusions.

[g024] medsurg
Which finding is most consistent with left-sided heart failure?
- Jugular vein distension
- Ascites
* Crackles in the lungs and dyspnea
- Ankle edema
> Left-sided failure backs fluid into the lungs. Right-sided failure causes systemic congestion such as edema, JVD and hepatomegaly.

[g025] medsurg
Which blood test is most specific for myocardial injury?

* Troponin
- Albumin
> Cardiac troponins rise within hours of myocardial injury and are the preferred biomarker.

[g026] medsurg
A patient with an acute ischemic stroke may receive IV thrombolysis (alteplase) if:
- Symptoms started more than 24 hours ago
* A CT scan rules out hemorrhage and treatment can start within about 4.5 hours of symptom onset
- The patient has a recent major surgery
- Blood pressure is 220/130 mmHg and untreated
> Time is brain. Hemorrhage must be excluded first, and eligibility criteria and blood pressure limits must be met.

[g027] medsurg

- 100%
- 96% to 100%

- 75% to 80%
> Controlled oxygen avoids worsening hypercapnia while preventing hypoxemia.

[g028] medsurg
In a chest drainage system, continuous bubbling in the water-seal chamber usually indicates:
- Normal function
* An air leak in the system or from the patient
- Re-expansion of the lung is complete
- The suction is turned off
> Gentle tidaling is normal. Continuous bubbling suggests an air leak; check connections from the patient to the unit.

[g029] medsurg
After a thyroidectomy, the patient reports tingling around the mouth and fingers. The nurse should suspect:
- Hyperkalemia
* Hypocalcemia from parathyroid injury
- Hypernatremia
- Thyroid storm only
> Check for Chvostek's and Trousseau's signs and have IV calcium gluconate available.

[g030] medsurg
Which ECG change is classic for hyperkalemia?
* Tall, peaked T waves
- Prominent U waves
- ST depression only
- Prolonged QT from hypocalcemia only
> Hyperkalemia causes peaked T waves and can progress to wide QRS and dangerous arrhythmias. U waves suggest hypokalemia.

[g031] medsurg

- 5.5 to 7.0 mmol/L
- 135 to 145 mmol/L
> Normal potassium is about 3.5–5.0 mmol/L. (135–145 mmol/L is the normal sodium range.)

[g032] medsurg
Which intervention best helps prevent atelectasis after abdominal surgery?

[g033] medsurg
Which finding most suggests deep vein thrombosis?

- Generalized itching

[g034] medsurg

[g035] medsurg

[g036] medsurg

[g037] medsurg

[g038] medsurg

[g039] medsurg

- Normal sleep

[g040] medsurg
Which is an early sign of increasing intracranial pressure?
* A change in level of consciousness
- Fixed, dilated pupils
- Cushing's triad
- Decerebrate posturing
> Changes in LOC (restlessness, confusion) are the earliest signs. Cushing's triad and fixed pupils are late signs.

[g041] pharm

- Check the radial pulse for 15 seconds
* Check the apical pulse for one full minute and withhold if below 60 (or per order)
- Give it with potassium supplements always
- Give it only at night
> Also monitor potassium levels, because hypokalemia increases the risk of digoxin toxicity.

* Nausea, vomiting and visual disturbances such as yellow-green halos
- High fever and rash
- Excessive thirst and urination

[g043] pharm

[g048] pharm

The first step when approaching an unresponsive adult in a hospital corridor is to:
* Check that the scene is safe, then check responsiveness and call for help
- Start rescue breaths immediately
- Move the patient to a bed
- Leave to find a doctor
> Scene safety, responsiveness, call for help/AED, then check breathing and pulse, and start CPR if needed.
