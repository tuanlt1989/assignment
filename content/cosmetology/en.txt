# Cosmetology theory bank — English master file.
# Original practice questions covering published cosmetology exam topics.

[c001] infection
What is the correct order of decontamination levels, from lowest to highest?
* Cleaning, disinfecting, sterilizing
- Sterilizing, cleaning, disinfecting
- Disinfecting, cleaning, sterilizing
- Cleaning, sterilizing, disinfecting
> Cleaning removes visible debris, disinfecting kills most microorganisms on hard surfaces, and sterilizing destroys all microbial life including spores.

[c002] infection
Quaternary ammonium compounds ("quats") are a type of:
- Antiseptic for the skin
* Disinfectant for hard, non-porous surfaces
- Hair conditioner
- Shampoo surfactant only
> Quats are common salon disinfectants. Use them only on tools and surfaces, at the dilution and contact time on the label.

[c003] infection
Combs and brushes should be prepared for the next client by:
- Spraying them with hairspray
* Removing all hair, washing with soap and water, then disinfecting
- Wiping them with a dry towel
- Rinsing them in hot water only
> Hair and debris must be removed before disinfection so the disinfectant can reach every surface.

[c004] infection
Disinfectants should never be used on:
- Countertops
- Shears
- Combs
* Human skin, hair or nails
> Disinfectants are for non-living surfaces. Antiseptics are the products made to reduce germs on skin.

[c005] infection
An antiseptic is a product designed to be used on:
* Skin, to reduce microbes
- Floors and countertops
- Metal implements only
- Electrical tools
> Antiseptics are milder than disinfectants and can be applied to skin. They do not replace disinfecting tools.

[c006] infection
Which scalp condition is contagious, so the service should not be performed?
- Dandruff (pityriasis)
- Gray hair (canities)
* Ringworm of the scalp (tinea capitis)
- Split ends (trichoptilosis)
> Tinea capitis is a contagious fungal infection. Do not service the client and suggest they see a physician.

[c007] infection
An infestation of head lice is called:
- Scabies
* Pediculosis capitis
- Tinea capitis
- Alopecia areata
> Head lice are contagious parasites. Do not perform the service; refer the client to a physician or pharmacist.

[c008] infection
Scabies is a contagious skin disease caused by:
- Bacteria
- A virus
* Itch mites burrowing under the skin
- A fungus
> Scabies is caused by mites. The client must be referred to a physician and should not receive services.

[c009] infection
Immunity gained after recovering from a disease or through vaccination is called:
- Natural immunity
* Acquired immunity
- Local immunity
- Temporary infection
> Acquired immunity develops after exposure or vaccination. Natural immunity is partly inherited.

[c010] infection
Implements that touch blood or body fluids must be:
- Rinsed under running water
* Cleaned, then disinfected with an EPA-registered disinfectant as your state requires
- Wiped with a towel and reused
- Placed in a drawer
> Clean first, then fully immerse in an EPA-registered disinfectant approved for blood exposure. Follow your state's rules exactly.

[c011] infection
Used towels and capes should be:
- Folded and reused on the next client
* Placed in a closed container until laundered
- Hung on the station chair
- Shaken out and reused
> Use clean linens for every client. Store soiled linens in a covered container and launder them properly.

[c012] infection
The main purpose of OSHA is to:
- Register disinfectants
- Issue cosmetology licenses
* Protect employees from hazards in the workplace
- Approve cosmetic products
> OSHA sets and enforces workplace safety standards, including the Hazard Communication Standard (SDS and labeling).

[c013] infection
Unlike many bacterial infections, viral infections:
- Are always cured by antibiotics
* Are not treated with antibiotics
- Cannot be spread to other people
- Only affect hair
> Antibiotics work on bacteria, not viruses. Vaccines and antiviral medicines are used against viruses.

[c014] infection
Mixing a disinfectant stronger than the label directs:
- Makes it work twice as fast
- Is required for blood spills
* Does not improve it and can damage tools and irritate skin
- Makes it safe to use on skin
> Always measure exactly. More concentrate does not mean better disinfection.

[c015] infection
Disposable gloves should be:
* Changed between clients and discarded after use
- Washed and reused
- Worn all day for every client
- Used only for haircuts
> Gloves are single-use. Change them between clients and wash your hands after removing them.

[c016] infection
When mixing concentrated disinfectant, you should:
- Use your bare hands to stir
* Wear gloves and safety glasses
- Mix it in a food container
- Add it to hot styling tools
> Concentrates can burn skin and eyes. Protect yourself and follow the label.

[c017] infection
A UL (Underwriters Laboratories) mark on an electrical appliance means:
- It is waterproof
* It has been tested and certified for safety
- It can be used without a plug
- It never needs inspection
> Use only UL-certified appliances and check cords for damage before each use.

[c018] infection
If a chemical splashes into a client's eyes, you should first:
- Rub the eyes with a towel
- Apply eye drops and continue the service
* Flush the eyes with plenty of clean water and seek medical help as needed
- Apply neutralizer
> Immediately flush with water for several minutes. Check the product's SDS first-aid section and get medical help.

[c019] infection

* A neck strip
- A metal comb
- Shears
- Plastic sectioning clips
> Neck strips and other porous items are single-use. Hard, non-porous tools are cleaned and disinfected.

[c020] infection

- Kept at the manufacturer only
* Available to employees for every hazardous product in the salon
- Read only by the salon owner
- Thrown away after one year

[c021] hair

- Collagen
- Melanin
* Keratin
- Elastin
> Hair is about 90% keratin. Keratin is also found in skin and nails.

[c022] hair

* Cuticle, cortex, medulla
- Medulla, cortex, cuticle
- Cortex, cuticle, medulla
- Follicle, bulb, papilla
> The cuticle protects, the cortex provides strength and color, and the medulla (the innermost layer) may be absent in fine hair.

[c023] hair
Which layer of the hair gives it strength, elasticity and natural color?
- Cuticle
* Cortex
- Medulla
- Follicle
> The cortex contains melanin and most of the bonds that chemical services change.

[c024] hair

* Cuticle
- Cortex
- Medulla
- Dermal papilla
> The cuticle is made of overlapping scales. A smooth, closed cuticle reflects light and looks shiny.

[c025] hair
The active growth phase of the hair cycle is:
- Catagen
- Telogen
* Anagen
- Exogen
> Anagen is the growth phase, catagen is the short transition phase, and telogen is the resting phase.

[c026] hair
On average, scalp hair grows about:
- 1/10 inch per month
* 1/2 inch (about 1.25 cm) per month
- 2 inches per month
- 1/2 inch per year
> Average growth is about 1/2 inch per month, varying with age, health and genetics.

[c027] hair
Hair porosity is the hair's ability to:
- Stretch and return to its length
* Absorb moisture
- Grow faster
- Hold a curl in humid weather
> Porous hair absorbs liquids quickly. Porosity affects how hair takes color, perms and relaxers.

[c028] hair
Hair elasticity is the ability of hair to:
* Stretch and return to its original length without breaking
- Absorb moisture
- Resist color
- Grow in a pattern
> Healthy wet hair can stretch roughly up to 50% of its length and return. Poor elasticity means weak hair.

[c029] hair
Hair texture refers to:
- The number of hairs per square inch
* The thickness or diameter of a single hair strand
- The color of the hair
- The direction of hair growth
> Texture is classified as coarse, medium or fine. Coarse hair often takes longer to process.

[c030] hair
Hair density refers to:
- The diameter of each hair
* The number of hairs per square inch of scalp
- How quickly hair grows
- How much moisture hair absorbs
> Density is described as thin, medium or thick, and it affects how you section and cut.

[c031] hair
Which side bonds are broken by water or heat and re-form when the hair dries or cools?
* Hydrogen bonds
- Disulfide bonds
- Peptide bonds
- Ionic bonds only
> Wet sets and thermal styling change hydrogen bonds temporarily. They re-form as hair dries or cools.

[c032] hair
Disulfide bonds in the hair are broken by:
- Shampooing
- Brushing
* Chemical services such as permanent waves and relaxers
- Cool water
> Disulfide bonds are strong chemical bonds. Perms and relaxers change them to permanently change the hair's shape.

[c033] hair
The medical term for dandruff is:
- Canities
* Pityriasis
- Trichoptilosis
- Monilethrix
> Pityriasis (dandruff) is excessive shedding of scalp skin cells. It is not considered contagious for salon purposes.

[c034] hair
Sudden hair loss in round or irregular patches is called:
* Alopecia areata
- Androgenetic alopecia
- Canities
- Hypertrichosis
> Alopecia areata is an autoimmune condition. Refer the client to a physician; be supportive.

[c035] hair
Hereditary hair loss known as male- or female-pattern baldness is:
- Alopecia areata
- Traction alopecia
* Androgenetic alopecia
- Tinea capitis
> Androgenetic alopecia is the most common type of hair loss and is influenced by genetics and hormones.

[c036] hair
Gray hair caused by the loss of natural pigment is called:
- Hirsutism
* Canities
- Pityriasis
- Fragilitas crinium
> Canities is the technical term for gray hair. Gray hair has normal growth but lacks melanin.

[c037] hair
Split ends are technically called:
- Monilethrix
- Trichorrhexis nodosa
* Trichoptilosis
- Pediculosis
> Trichoptilosis is split ends. Trimming and conditioning help; they cannot be permanently repaired.

[c038] hair
The small cone-shaped area at the base of the follicle that supplies nourishment to the hair bulb is the:
- Arrector pili
* Dermal papilla
- Sebaceous gland
- Cuticle
> The dermal papilla contains blood vessels and nerves that nourish the hair bulb.

[c039] hair
The tiny muscle attached to the hair follicle that causes "goosebumps" is the:
* Arrector pili
- Occipitalis
- Platysma
- Frontalis
> The arrector pili contracts with cold or fear, making the hair stand up.

[c040] hair
The two types of melanin in hair are:
- Keratin and collagen
* Eumelanin (brown/black) and pheomelanin (red/yellow)
- Sebum and sweat
- Cortex and medulla
> The mix of eumelanin and pheomelanin creates each person's natural hair color.

[c041] color
The primary colors are:
- Orange, green, violet
* Red, yellow, blue
- Red, green, blue
- Black, white, gray
> Primary colors cannot be created by mixing other colors. All other colors come from them.

[c042] color
Mixing equal parts of yellow and blue creates:
- Orange
- Violet
* Green
- Brown
> Green is a secondary color made from yellow and blue.

[c043] color
Which color neutralizes (cancels out) unwanted red tones?
- Orange
* Green
- Red-violet
- Yellow
> Complementary colors neutralize each other. Green is opposite red on the color wheel.

[c044] color
To neutralize unwanted orange (brassy) tones, use a toner or color with a base of:
* Blue
- Red
- Yellow
- Orange
> Blue is the complement of orange. Violet neutralizes yellow; green neutralizes red.

[c045] color
In haircoloring, the level system measures:
- How warm or cool a color is
* How light or dark a color is
- How porous the hair is
- How long color lasts
> Most level systems range from 1 (black, darkest) to 10 (lightest blonde).

[c046] color
The "tone" of a hair color describes its:
- Lightness or darkness
* Warmth or coolness
- Permanence
- Developer strength
> Tones are warm (gold, red, orange), cool (ash, blue, violet, green) or neutral.

[c047] color
Temporary hair color:
* Coats the outside of the hair and is removed with one shampoo
- Penetrates the cortex and lasts until it grows out
- Requires 30-volume developer
- Lifts natural color two levels
> Temporary colors are large molecules that stay on the cuticle surface. No patch test is usually needed.

[c048] color
Traditional semi-permanent hair color:
- Lightens hair several levels
* Deposits color without developer and fades over several shampoos
- Is always mixed with 40-volume developer
- Cannot cover any gray
> Semi-permanent color is deposit-only and gradually fades. Many still require a patch test.

[c049] color
Demi-permanent hair color is mixed with:
- No developer at all
* A low-volume developer, and it deposits color without lifting
- 40-volume developer for maximum lift
- Bleach powder
> Demi-permanent color uses a low-volume developer, deposits color and gives some gray blending without lightening.

[c050] color
Permanent (oxidation) hair color:
- Only coats the cuticle
- Washes out in one shampoo
* Can lighten and deposit color in the same process
- Never needs a patch test
> Permanent color is mixed with developer, penetrates the cortex, and can lift and deposit at the same time.

[c051] color
20-volume developer contains approximately what percentage of hydrogen peroxide?
- 3%
* 6%
- 9%
- 12%
> 10 volume = 3%, 20 volume = 6%, 30 volume = 9%, 40 volume = 12%.

[c052] color
A patch test (predisposition test) for aniline-derivative tints should be done:
- Right after the color service
* 24 to 48 hours before the color service
- Only once in a client's lifetime
- Only on clients with gray hair
> The patch test checks for allergies. If there is redness, swelling, burning or itching, do not apply the color.

[c053] color
A strand test is used to:
- Check for allergies
* Determine processing time and how the color will look before the full application
- Clean the scalp
- Measure density
> The strand test shows the result on a small section so you can adjust the formula or timing.

[c054] color
Hair lightener (bleach) lightens hair by:
* Diffusing and dissolving melanin through oxidation
- Coating the cuticle with white pigment
- Breaking hydrogen bonds only
- Removing the medulla
> Lighteners oxidize natural melanin. The longer and stronger the process, the more pigment is removed.

[c055] color
As dark hair is lightened, it passes through which sequence of stages?
- Yellow, orange, red, brown
* Red, red-orange, orange, gold, yellow, pale yellow
- Pale yellow, gold, red, black
- Violet, blue, green, yellow
> Natural hair lightens through warm stages from red toward pale yellow. Toners then adjust the final tone.

[c056] color
For a virgin application of permanent color to go lighter, you usually apply color:
- To the scalp area first
* To the mid-shaft and ends first, then to the scalp area
- To the ends only
- All at once to dry hair, without sections
> Body heat makes the scalp area process faster, so it is applied last to give even results.

[c057] color
A color retouch is applied to:
* The new growth only
- The ends only
- The entire hair shaft every time
- The eyebrows
> Overlapping previously colored hair can cause breakage and color bands. Apply only to the regrowth.

[c058] color
Color fillers are used to:
- Remove artificial color
* Equalize porosity and deposit a color base in damaged hair
- Lighten hair faster
- Replace the patch test
> Fillers help damaged or porous hair take color evenly and prevent results that are too dark or ashy.

[c059] color
Hair previously colored with metallic dyes should:
- Be lightened immediately
* Be tested for compatibility before any chemical service
- Be permed without testing
- Be relaxed with a hydroxide relaxer
> Metallic dyes can react with peroxide and other chemicals, causing heat, breakage or discoloration.

[c060] color
To remove artificial (oxidative) color from the hair, you would use a:
- Patch test
* Dye solvent (color remover)
- Demi-permanent color
- Neutralizing shampoo
> Dye solvents shrink artificial color molecules so they can be rinsed out.

[c061] chemical
The main active ingredient in an alkaline (cold) wave is:
* Ammonium thioglycolate
- Glyceryl monothioglycolate
- Sodium hydroxide
- Hydrogen peroxide
> Alkaline waves have a higher pH, process quickly at room temperature and give firm curls.

[c062] chemical
The main active ingredient in a true acid wave is:
- Ammonium thioglycolate
* Glyceryl monothioglycolate (GMTG)
- Guanidine hydroxide
- Sodium bicarbonate
> Acid waves have a lower pH and give softer curls. GMTG is a common sensitizer, so protect skin.

[c063] chemical
Permanent waving solution softens the hair by breaking:
- Hydrogen bonds only
* Disulfide bonds
- Peptide bonds
- Salt bonds only
> The waving solution breaks disulfide bonds so the hair takes the shape of the rod.

[c064] chemical
Permanent wave neutralizer contains hydrogen peroxide to:
- Break more disulfide bonds
* Rebuild the disulfide bonds in their new curled shape
- Lighten the hair
- Soften the cuticle
> Neutralizing (oxidation) re-forms the broken disulfide bonds and stops the processing.

[c065] chemical
In permanent waving, the size of the curl is mainly determined by:
* The size of the rod
- The brand of solution
- The length of the end paper
- The water temperature
> Smaller rods make smaller, tighter curls; larger rods make larger, looser curls.

[c066] chemical
End papers are used during perm wrapping to:
- Speed up processing
* Control the ends and prevent fishhooks
- Protect the scalp from neutralizer
- Measure curl size
> End papers keep the ends smooth around the rod so they do not bend or crimp.

[c067] chemical
A test curl is taken during a permanent wave to:
* Check how the hair is processing and when it is fully processed
- Check for allergies
- Remove the end papers
- Measure the hair's density
> Test curls help prevent under- and over-processing.

[c068] chemical
An over-processed permanent wave usually looks:
- Tight and springy when dry
* Frizzy when wet and weak or straight when dry
- Exactly the same as the rod size
- Too shiny
> Over-processing breaks too many bonds. The hair loses strength and cannot hold a firm curl.

[c069] chemical
Sodium hydroxide relaxers are commonly called:
- No-lye relaxers
* Lye relaxers
- Thio relaxers
- Acid relaxers
> Sodium hydroxide relaxers have a very high pH and work quickly. "No-lye" relaxers use other hydroxides such as guanidine.

[c070] chemical
Hydroxide relaxers permanently straighten hair through a process called:
- Oxidation
- Neutralization
* Lanthionization
- Hydrolysis of hydrogen bonds
> Hydroxide relaxers convert disulfide bonds to lanthionine bonds, which cannot be re-formed into curls.

[c071] chemical
A protective base cream is applied before a base relaxer to:
* Protect the scalp from irritation and burns
- Make the relaxer process faster
- Add color to the hair
- Replace the neutralizing shampoo
> Base cream (petroleum-based) protects the scalp and hairline from the high-pH relaxer.

[c072] chemical
After thoroughly rinsing a hydroxide relaxer, you must:
- Apply a thio neutralizer
* Use a neutralizing (normalizing) shampoo to restore the hair's acid pH
- Apply a perm solution
- Leave the hair as it is
> The neutralizing shampoo lowers the pH and removes any remaining relaxer. Many have a color indicator.

[c073] chemical
Hair that has been relaxed with a hydroxide relaxer:
- Can safely be given a thio relaxer or perm
* Should not receive a thio relaxer or permanent wave
- Must be lightened before any service
- Will curl again after shampooing
> Hydroxide and thio chemicals are not compatible. Mixing them can cause severe breakage.

[c074] chemical
Many keratin smoothing treatments can release formaldehyde when heated, so you should:
- Use them only on wet hair at maximum heat
* Ensure good ventilation and follow the manufacturer's instructions and SDS
- Skip gloves because the product is natural
- Apply them only in closed rooms
> Formaldehyde releasers can irritate eyes and lungs. Ventilation and correct use reduce exposure.

[c075] chemical
If a client has scalp abrasions or open sores, you should:
- Use a stronger relaxer to finish quickly
* Not perform a chemical service
- Apply extra base cream and continue
- Use hot water to clean the area

- 7.0
